# `lisploop` — plan 7

## Overview: Aim, Design Principle, and Milestone Map

### Aim

`lisploop` is motivated by & tests the hypothesis: **there are many levels of conservation, some of which are generated from different views of the same data and meta-data, especially different forms of causality. When conserved information or views are destroyed, hidden, or aliased, a fixed set of algorithms for inferring them suffices - and can bootstrap inferring algorithms, heuristics, languages, search policies, and search messages/constraints that can infer or find them**.

It is implemented as a self-contained Common Lisp kernel and experimental suite over finite prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ and unbounded signed integers $\mathbb{Z}$. 
The system investigates how automated mathematical and algorithmic discovery arises from three interacting components
(below, [M1](#sec-m1)–[M9](#sec-m9) refer to the milestones summarized in the [Reading & Implementation Map](#sec-reading-map) and defined in [Section 4](#sec-milestones); concrete representations and mathematical nomenclature are defined in [Section 1](#sec-shared-contract) and [Section 2](#sec-nomenclature)):

1. **Symmetry (Conservation Under Action and Across Observations)**: Discovery begins by detecting what remains invariant when inputs, states, or syntax are transformed. At the empirical data level, a synthesized program $e(X)$ conserves a target input-output relation $A_\omega(Y)$ across observation rows $\omega \in \Omega$ ([M1](#sec-m1)), while an action invariant $h(X)$ is conserved along the trajectory steps of a state transition $T$ (`pairs`) yet varies across distinct orbits (`anchors`, [M2](#sec-m2)) and hence remains informative. At the operator and meta-data level, universal algebraic laws $e_1(X) = e_2(X)$ and domain-validity guards $G(X)$ are conserved across an entire domain grid $D^\Gamma$ ([M3A](#sec-m3)), and shared syntactic skeletons $p$ are conserved across corpora of laws, subexpressions, or prime moduli ([M3B](#sec-m3), [M6](#sec-m6), [M8A](#sec-m8)).
2. **Algebra (Converting Conserved Expressions into Generative Models, Descriptions, and Backward Solvers)**: A conserved relation $R(X, Y)$ or invariant $h(s) = h(Ts)$ is initially a passive test—it checks whether a given tuple is valid, but does not by itself generate solutions. Algebra turns passive conservation into **active generation and backward constraint propagation**: reorienting which coordinates of a recorded relation serve as known inputs vs. unknown targets (`orient-observations`, `orient-arithmetic-requirements`), synthesizing explicit witness functions $q(K, C)$ and solvability guards $G(K, C)$ that invert forward operators ([M4](#sec-m4)), and pulling parent requirements $A_\omega(Y)$ backward through outer decoders $Y = d(X, Z)$ to generate simplified child synthesis problems (`pullback-frame`, `propagate-arithmetic-child`).
3. **Interacting Views of Data & Meta-Data, and Inferring Hidden or Aliased Information**: Because object-language programs, row requirement predicates $A_\omega(Y)$, algebraic laws, and anti-unification templates are all homoiconic S-expressions, the system can project both runtime data and symbolic meta-data through interchangeable views: deduplicated lookup tables (quotient views), syntactic templates with holes ([M3B](#sec-m3), [M6](#sec-m6)), synthesized input-feature projections (`start-dependency-search`), and **piecewise regimes**—subsets of observations $\Omega_k \subseteq \Omega$ that follow different local rules or parameters when a single uniform formula does not fit all rows. Specifically, a regime split may be either an **observable Boolean regime split** ([M7](#sec-m7)), where a synthesized guard predicate $G(X)$ partitions the input space into `(if G branch_1 branch_2)` (e.g., even vs. odd episodes), or a **latent regime parameter** $a_{j(r)}$ ([M5](#sec-m5)), where an unobserved intermediate value $Z = a_{j(r)}$ (such as a hidden per-episode offset $a_0 = 1$ vs. $a_1 = 3$ inside $y_{r,t} = \iota(t) + a_{j(r)}$) switches across occurrence groups $r$ without any explicit regime label $j(r)$ in the data. Whenever a projection or many-to-one operator **aliases** distinct states onto the same observable value or hides an unlabelled coordinate—such as $0 \cdot Z = 0$, truncating integer division $Z / 2 = 1 \implies \{2, 3\}$, latent regime parameters $a_{j(r)}$ in [M5](#sec-m5), or shared subexpressions buried at varying tree paths in [M6](#sec-m6)—the kernel infers or resolves the hidden structure via conservative requirement folding (`fold-constants`), bounded fiber partition proposals (`infer-fibers`), explicit dependency/selector synthesis (`start-dependency-search`), structural conflict pruning (`frame-conflict-p`), and subgraph correspondence search (`discover-subexpression-abstractions`).

### Core Design Principle

Every capability across all nine milestones is governed by two structural invariants:

1. **Homoiconic S-expression uniformity**: All mathematical objects in the system—object-language programs, row requirement predicates $A_\omega(Y)$ and $B_\omega(Z)$, quoted syntax values of type `(:code inputs sort)`, anti-unification templates with holes `#:?0`, algebraic laws, and rewrite rules—are ordinary Lisp cons trees constructed from a pooled symbol table (`*typed-symbol-pool*`) with $O(1)$ precomputed prefix-free bit-size metadata stored in a weak `eq` hash table (`*node-meta*`).
2. **Two-resource accounting ($S$ vs. $W$), intermediate "mountain passes," distributed costing, and search policy**: The **end goal** of discovery is to minimize a joint objective of **Description Length ($S$)** and **Computational Work ($W$)**—at the model level $J(M) = S(M) + S(\varepsilon(M, \Omega)) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(M)$ and at the procedural level $J_{\mathrm{procedure}}(P) = J(M_P) + \lambda_{\mathrm{search}} W_{\mathrm{search}}(P)$. However, **individual transformations along a synthesis pathway do not reduce $S$ or $W$ monotonically; search must frequently "go over mountain passes" that temporarily expand representation size or pay upfront work in order to make downstream computation or synthesis easier**:
   - *Representation-expanding intermediate steps*: Pulling a compact singleton requirement `(= Y 'y_w)` back through an outer decoder $Y = d(X, Z)$ (`pullback-frame`) or reorienting an arithmetic relation (`orient-arithmetic-requirements`) expands each row's requirement into a larger relational predicate $B_\omega(Z)$ and introduces extra context bindings (`bindings`), increasing intermediate syntax size to expose a simpler child target $Z$. Similarly, introducing a Boolean regime guard `(if G branch_1 branch_2)` (`discover-partition-views`), an anti-unification template $p$, or a latent regime coordinate $j(r)$ (`infer-fibers`, `context-label-frame`) pays upfront header or selector bits that only yield a net reduction in $S$ once the downstream child subproblems compress; and checked equational rewriting (`apply-checked-rewrite` in `enumerate.lisp`) retains larger and non-canonical expressions alongside canonical ones because an expanded expression may have lower runtime work $W_{\mathrm{run}}$ or expose a shared template for anti-unification ([M6](#sec-m6) Stage 5).
   - *Distributed rule costing and parent-level reconstruction pricing*: Because local steps can expand before compressing, **rule application and costing are distributed**: each local step (`pullback-frame`, `fold-constants`, `propagate-arithmetic-child`, `apply-checked-rewrite`, `infer-fibers`, `evaluate-guard-partition`) charges its local work bucket into the shared `ledger` as it executes, while child searches retain **all** verified child proposals (`search-job-proposals`) so that description length $S$ and runtime work $W_{\mathrm{run}}$ are priced on the **fully reassembled parent expression** $M_{\mathrm{parent}}(X) = d(X, e_C(e_0(X), \dots))$ (`compose-model`, `verify-parent-reconstruction`, `price-view-combinations` in `synthesize.lisp`).
   - *Branch-selection policy (currently implicit 3:1 round-robin, explicitly learned in [M8D](#sec-m8))*: Deciding *which* decomposition branch, rewrite, or shortcut lane to allocate work quanta to next is the **search policy**. In the current Lisp kernel (`synthesize.lisp`, `laws.lisp`, `factor.lisp`), this policy is **fixed and implicit**: active decomposition branches (`synthesis-node`s) or locality-ordered occurrence pairs (`schedule-occurrence-pairs`) are stepped in a deterministic round-robin queue that spends **3 out of every 4 work steps** on the decomposed/shortcut lane while reserving **1 out of every 4 steps** (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`) for plain exhaustive root search as a completeness backup (see [3:1 Interleaved Scheduling](#sec-3-to-1-scheduling)), with every candidate re-checked against the original root specification. Replacing the implicit round-robin node order with a learned symbolic continuation-value policy is the [M8D](#sec-m8) extension.
   All nine milestones are built by composing four fundamental operations along these pathways:
   - **Operation 1 — Observational Conservation Across Frame Rows**: Unary synthesis, cross-row empirical equivalence and action invariance, exhaustive law certification, and exact Boolean guard synthesis.
   - **Operation 2 — Factorization & Latent Fiber Proposals ($S$ Compression)**: Right-inverses of `Apply` across syntax trees (`anti-unify`, subgraph correspondence), value tables (`quotient-values`, `partition-rows`), and pulled-back requirement fibers (`infer-fibers` via bounded lexicographic MDL partition search).
   - **Operation 3 — Causal Reorientation, Contravariant Pullback, Conservative Constant Folding & Dual-Grade Propagation**: Reorienting relational observations without re-simulation, pulling parent requirements $A_\omega(Y)$ back through decoders $Y = d(X, Z)$ via `fold-constants` (preserving partial/error semantics), detecting conflicts via `frame-conflict-p`, and narrowing child requirements via certified ($\iff$) in-place replacement or tentative ($\Leftarrow$) [3:1 scheduled propagation](#sec-3-to-1-scheduling).
   - **Operation 4 — Lossy Heuristic Condensation, Dependency Selection & Multi-Lane Search Control ($W$ Compression)**: Synthesizing compact heuristic programs $\hat{\pi}(X)$ via resumable `start-heuristic-search` / `condense-heuristic` (pricing residuals during training and dropping the residual table at deployment) and competing explicit dependency/selector hypotheses (`start-dependency-search`) or recursive decompositions (`start-synthesis`) under the [3:1 interleaved schedule](#sec-3-to-1-scheduling).

## 1. Shared Contract: Homoiconic Representation, Availability, Cost Accounting & Epistemic Grades {#sec-shared-contract}

All nine milestones share a single representation, execution engine, cost ledger, and epistemic grading contract. No milestone may introduce a private evaluator, unpriced side channel, or unverified shortcut.

### 1.1 Ambient Numeric Domains, Semantic Types, and Schema $\Gamma$

Every synthesis, observation, or validation problem is governed by an explicit **Schema** $\Gamma$:
$$\Gamma = \bigl(D,\; X_0{:}\tau_0,\, \dots,\, X_{m-1}{:}\tau_{m-1} \to \tau\bigr)$$
constructed by `make-schema` in `ast.lisp` (`schema-inputs`, `schema-target`, `schema-allow-lookup-p`):

1. **Ambient Numeric Domain $D$**: Either unbounded signed integers `:int` ($\mathbb{Z}$) or a prime finite field `(:gf p)` ($\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p$ verified prime at construction by `prime-modulus-p`; composite moduli such as `(gf 4)` raise an immediate error). All arithmetic inside $\mathbb{F}_p$ is reduced modulo $p$, with exact multiplicative inverse via extended GCD (`gf-invert`) and division by zero raising a metered `evaluation-error`. In `:int`, division `:/` is truncating toward zero (`truncate`), and any non-Boolean conditionals or out-of-bounds sequence/tuple operations raise `evaluation-error`.
2. **Semantic Types $\tau$ and Runtime Value Sets $\mathcal{V}_\tau$**:
   - **Scalars**: `:int` ($\mathcal{V}_{\texttt{:int}} = \mathbb{Z}$), `(:gf p)` ($\mathcal{V}_{\texttt{(:gf }p\texttt{)}} = \{0, \dots, p-1\}$), and `:bool` ($\mathcal{V}_{\texttt{:bool}} = \{\texttt{nil}, \texttt{t}\}$).
   - **Product Types**: `(:tuple tau_0 ... tau_{k-1})` represented at runtime as proper lists `(v_0 ... v_{k-1})` of length $k$. Homogeneous tuples `(:tuple tau ... tau)` can be indexed by non-negative `:int` positions via `:at` with the exact same bounds-checked semantics as sequences.
   - **Sequence Types**: `(:seq elem-type)` represented at runtime as proper lists of homogeneous elements, indexed by scalar non-negative `:int` positions via `:at`, batch index lists via `:gather`, or key-value tuple tables via `:lookup`.
   - **Quoted Syntax Types**: `(:code ((id_0 . tau_0) ...) sort)` whose runtime values $v \in \mathcal{V}_\tau$ are typed object-language ASTs wrapped as `(lit code-type expr)`. Quoted syntax values are inspected and constructed by structural `:code-*` operators (`:code-kind`, `:code-child`, `:code-operator`, `:code-apply`, `:code-match`, `:code-bind`, `:code-subst`, `:code-total`, `:code-eval`) without ever invoking host `eval` or `funcall`.
3. **Exhaustive Domain Grid $D^\Gamma$**: When every input type $\tau_r$ is finite (`(:gf p)`, `:bool`, or nested tuples thereof), the exhaustive input grid $D^\Gamma = \mathcal{V}_{\tau_0} \times \dots \times \mathcal{V}_{\tau_{m-1}}$ is enumerable (`enumerable-type-p`, `enumerate-domain-rows`), enabling complete finite-domain law validation.

### 1.2 Homoiconic S-Expression AST and $O(1)$ Metadata Cache

Programs, requirements, laws, and anti-unification patterns are represented directly as canonical S-expressions rather than parallel `defstruct` hierarchies (`ast.lisp`):

- **Variables**: `(var id type)` produces a pooled uninterned symbol `#:X0`, `#:X1`, ... from `*typed-symbol-pool*` whose `ast-meta` stores `:kind :var`, `:id id`, `:type type`, `:non-var-bits 2`, `:var-count 1`, `:syntax-work 1`, `:total-p t`, and `:free-vars (list id)`.
- **Holes**: `(hole id type :kind (:value | :fn))` produces `#:?0`, `#:?1`, ... from `*typed-symbol-pool*` with `:kind :hole` (or `:fn-hole` when `:kind :fn`).
- **Operators**: `(op name sig &key arity)` produces a pooled operator symbol (e.g., `#:+`, `#:if`, `#:tuple`, `#:at`, `#:gather`, `#:lookup`, `#:code-match`) carrying `:kind :op`, `:id name`, and `:type (cons arg-types return-type)`.
- **Literals**: `(lit type value)` constructs a fresh 2-element cons `(#:QUOTE copied-value)` headed by the pooled `quote-symbol` (`#:QUOTE`, printed as `'value` via `set-pprint-dispatch`) and registers its `ast-meta` in the weak `eq` table `*node-meta*` with `:non-var-bits (+ 2 (value-bit-size copied-value type))` and `:var-count 0`.
- **Applications**: `(app op arg_1 ... arg_k)` constructs the ordinary list `(op arg_1 ... arg_k)` after verifying exact operator arity and argument types, immediately synthesizing and caching its `ast-meta` in `*node-meta*`:
  $$\mathrm{non\text{-}var\text{-}bits}\bigl((f\; e_1 \dots e_k)\bigr) = 2 + \mathrm{operator\text{-}bits}(f) + \sum_{i=1}^k \mathrm{non\text{-}var\text{-}bits}(e_i), \qquad \mathrm{var\text{-}count}\bigl((f\; e_1 \dots e_k)\bigr) = \sum_{i=1}^k \mathrm{var\text{-}count}(e_i).$$

Given any input signature $\Gamma_{\mathrm{inputs}} = ((id_0 . \tau_0) \dots (id_{m-1} . \tau_{m-1}))$, `body-size` and `description-size` (`codec.lisp`) validate variable scope and types via `expression-type` and compute the exact prefix-free body bit-length from the cached metadata:
$$S_{\mathrm{body}}(e, \Gamma) = \mathrm{non\text{-}var\text{-}bits}(e) + \mathrm{var\text{-}count}(e) \cdot \mathrm{integer\text{-}length}\bigl(\max(0, m - 1)\bigr).$$

### 1.3 Observation Frame $\Omega$, Row $\omega$, and Strict Information Isolation

- An **Observation Frame** $\Omega$ (`frame` in `frame.lisp`) combines a `schema` $\Gamma$ with a sequence of **Rows** $\omega \in \Omega$ (`row` in `frame.lisp`):
$$\omega = \bigl(\mathrm{key}(\omega),\; \mathbf{x}_\omega,\; A_\omega(Y)\bigr)$$
where:
  - $\mathrm{key}(\omega)$ (`row-key`) is an explicit occurrence identifier (such as a sample index, sequence position, or transition probe tag),
  - $\mathbf{x}_\omega = (x_{\omega,0}, \dots, x_{\omega,m-1}) \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_0} \times \dots \times \mathcal{V}_{\tau_{m-1}}$ (`row-known`) is the tuple of bound input values available at runtime, and
  - $A_\omega(Y)$ (`row-requirement`) is a **unary Boolean predicate S-expression** over the candidate output variable $Y = \texttt{(var 0 }\tau\texttt{)}$ (`#:X0` at runtime). It is *closed with respect to the row's input variables* $X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}$ because their bound runtime values $\mathbf{x}_\omega$ have already been substituted in as literals `'x_w`, leaving $Y = \texttt{(var 0 }\tau\texttt{)}$ as the sole free symbolic variable.
- **Execution in the VM Checker**: When search or model verification evaluates a candidate expression $e(X)$ on row $\omega$'s inputs $\mathbf{x}_\omega$ (charging `:proposal-execution` work) to obtain a proposed output value $\hat{y}_\omega = M(e, \omega) \in \mathcal{V}_\tau$, the checker (`start-requirement-evaluation` in `enumerate.lisp`, `check-requirement` in `frame.lisp`) binds `(list y_hat)` as the single-variable environment for `(var 0 tau)` and evaluates $A_\omega(\hat{y}_\omega)$ in the resumable VM (charging `:checker` work). Row $\omega$ passes if and only if $A_\omega(\hat{y}_\omega)$ evaluates to `'t`.
  - **Standard Forms & Lifecycle Across Contexts**: Depending on how the frame was constructed, $A_\omega(Y)$ takes one of four homoiconic forms:
    1. *Supervised / Witnessed I/O (`singleton-requirement`)*: `(= X0 'y_w)`, requiring the exact output $y_\omega$.
    2. *Multi-Witness / Non-Deterministic Reorientation (`set-requirement`)*: `(member X0 '(v_1 ... v_k))` (or `'t` if unconstrained, `'nil` if impossible), produced when `orient-observations` or `orient-arithmetic-requirements` reorients a relation where multiple target values correspond to the same projected input $\mathbf{x}_\omega$.
    3. *Support / Guard Indicator Projection (`support-frame` in M3A, `condense-heuristic` in M5/M7)*: `(= X0 't)` on rows where a candidate law or regime holds and `(= X0 'nil)` (or `(= X0 'j_w)`) on rows where it fails or switches regimes.
    4. *Pulled-Back & Conjoined Child Predicates ($B_\omega(Z)$ in M1, M4, M5, M7, M8)*: Arbitrary unary Boolean S-expressions over the child target $Z = \texttt{(var 0 }\tau_C\texttt{)}$—such as `(= (+ '4 X0) '1)`, `(= (/ X0 '2) '1)`, or conjoined fiber constraints `(if (= (/ X0 '2) '1) (= (/ X0 '3) '1) 'nil)`—created when a parent requirement $A_\omega(Y)$ is pulled back through an outer decoder $Y = d(X, Z)$ (`pullback-frame`), simplified (`fold-constants`), optionally narrowed by downward algebraic propagation (`propagate-arithmetic-child`), or intersected across rows in a latent cluster (`conjoin-requirements`, `infer-fibers`).

In addition to per-row unary requirements $A_\omega(Y)$, a `frame` $\Omega$ optionally carries relational row linkages `frame-pairs` and `frame-anchors` over row keys $\mathrm{key}(\omega)$, which can either be supplied from transition trajectories (`transition-probes`) or **computed** from any partition, view split, or fiber clustering (`partition-pairs-and-anchors`, `fiber-pairs-and-anchors`, `with-frame-linkage`).

**Strict Information Isolation ("No Informational Loops")**:

1. **Separation of Frame, Oracle, and Provenance**: The `frame` $\Omega$ contains only what the learner is allowed to observe. Any planted generating program or test fixture metadata is stored in a separate `provenance` structure that is never passed to search, law discovery, or factorization procedures. If an interactive `oracle` is used, it may only evaluate candidate inputs $\mathbf{x}$ and charge `:simulation` work; its internal syntax is inaccessible.
2. **Copy-on-Create Boundary Isolation**: Constructing an `observation-scope` or `search-job` deep-copies `frame-rows` and `language-literals` (`copy-row-list`, `copy-value`) so subsequent mutation by a caller cannot leak information into an active job.
3. **Standalone Model Execution as Ordinary Expressions**: Every synthesized `model` (`program-model`, `lookup-model`, `quotient-model`, `partition-model`, `corpus-abstraction-model`) carries an ordinary typed object-language expression in `model-program` (`lookup-model-program`, `quotient-model-program`, `partition-model-program`, `corpus-abstraction-model-program`), serializes to a self-delimiting bit vector via `encode-model`, decodes in a clean state via `decode-model`, and passes `verify-model` / `execute-model` after the originating `frame`, `oracle`, and `provenance` have been nilled out.

### 1.4 Single Resumable Evaluation Stepper and Scope-Tagged Return Channel

All expression evaluation—whether inside candidate synthesis, row requirement checking, observational bucketing, law validation, constant folding, or symbolic rewriting—executes through the single frame-stack state machine `evaluation` in `vm.lisp` (`start-evaluation`, `advance-evaluation`, `advance-accounted-evaluation`, `evaluate`):

1. **Debt-Draining Preemption (`evaluation-remaining-cost`)**: Every VM step checks both remaining budget (`<= budget 0`) and pending sub-step instruction debt (`> (evaluation-remaining-cost eval) 0`). When a heavy multi-word arithmetic operation (such as $2^{256} \times 2^{256}$) or a prepaid bounds check exceeds the current work quantum, its cost is registered in `evaluation-remaining-cost` and drained across subsequent `advance-evaluation` calls before the instruction's result is pushed to `evaluation-stack` or its error is signaled.
2. **Exact `quantum = 1` vs. Batch Parity**: Because instruction task continuations (`evaluation-tasks`), operand stacks (`evaluation-stack`), and work ledgers are updated atomically as debt drains, interrupting and resuming any search, observation, validation, propagation, heuristic, or recursive synthesis job with `quantum = 1` produces the exact same (`equalp`) continuation state, work ledger, and discovered results as running with a large batch budget.
3. **Scope-Tagged Return Channel (`message` and `scope-evidence`)**: Every search, validation, fiber-proposal, heuristic, or recursive synthesis driver returns a `message` struct (`ast.lisp`, `synthesize.lisp`) containing:
   - `message-status` $\in \{\texttt{:complete}, \texttt{:cutoff}, \texttt{:exhausted}, \texttt{:invalid}\}$,
   - `message-best`, `message-proposals`, `message-work`, `message-cursor`, `message-diagnostics`, and
   - explicit epistemic scope metadata: `message-call-id`, `message-claim` (e.g., `:search-result`, `:observation-table`, `:heuristic-condensation`, `:partition-proposal`, `:dependency-selection`, `:recursive-synthesis`, `:cutoff`), and `message-evidence` (`scope-evidence` recording `:kind`, `:domain`, `:inputs`, `:target`, `:row-count`, `:covers-domain-p`, `:size-limit`, `:work-limit`, `:feasibility`, and `:notes`).
   Any candidate whose evaluation or requirement check is interrupted by a budget cutoff remains unresolved in the job's continuation cursor and is never reported as verified or refuted.

### 1.5 Exact Cost Accounting ($S$, $W_{\mathrm{run}}$, $W_{\mathrm{search}}$) and Two-Level Objectives

#### Description Length $S$ (Encoding v7)
Every model $M$ is encoded into a prefix-free bit vector under **Encoding v7** (`+encoding-version+ = 7`, `codec.lisp`):
$$S(M) = S_{\mathrm{header}}(\Gamma) + S_{\mathrm{body}}(M, \Gamma)$$

- **Header $S_{\mathrm{header}}(\Gamma)$ (`header-size`)**: Encodes the 3-bit version (`7`), input count via `encode-natural`, each formal input type $\tau_r$ via `encode-type`, and target type $\tau$ via `encode-type` once per model. Formal variable IDs are mapped to 0-based positional indices within `inputs` so $S_{\mathrm{body}}(M, \Gamma)$ and enumeration order are completely invariant to raw symbol ID numbers.
- **Self-Delimiting Integers & Operator Opcodes**: Positive integers $n \ge 1$ use Elias delta coding (`positive-bit-size`: `(1- length-width)` zero bits + `length-width` bits of `width = (integer-length n)` + `(1- width)` low bits of $n$); naturals $n \ge 0$ use `natural-bit-size(n) = positive-bit-size(n + 1)`; signed integers use zigzag encoding (`signed-bit-size`). Operators use a 4-bit opcode index into `+operator-names+` (21 operators: 11 object-level `:+`, `:-`, `:*`, `:/`, `:=`, `:member`, `:if`, `:tuple`, `:at`, `:gather`, `:lookup` + 9 `:code-*` operators), with index $\ge 15$ writing `1111` followed by `positive-bit-size(index - 14)`.
- **Body $S_{\mathrm{body}}(M, \Gamma)$ (`body-size`)**: Uses 2-bit AST node tags (`0` `:var`, `1` `:lit`, `2` `:app`; tag `3` is rejected during expression decoding so holes cannot appear in serialized object-language programs, while AU tree corpora use `encode-tree-corpus` / `decode-tree-corpus`).
- **Analytical Literal Inversion**: `literal-range` in `codec.lisp` inverts the zigzag Elias-delta bit-size threshold in $O(1)$ time to determine the exact integer interval $[-2^{k-1}, 2^{k-1}-1]$ that fits within $b$ remaining bits during bounded bottom-up enumeration.

#### Computational Work $W$ (Tariff v9 & Simulator v5)
Every operation charges an explicit `ledger` (`+tariff-version+ = 9`, `+simulator-version+ = 5`, `ast.lisp`, `vm.lisp`) via `charge` and enforces hard step bounds via `with-work-limit` / `remaining-work`:

- **Core evaluation & search buckets**: `:enumeration` (1 unit per candidate AST production or literal step), `:proposal-execution` (evaluating a candidate $e$ on row inputs $\mathbf{x}_\omega$), `:checker` (evaluating requirement $A_\omega(Y)$ on the proposed output), `:simulation` (querying an external oracle or generating forward execution tables), `:orientation` (reorienting recorded observations, pulling back frames, or folding closed constants), `:rewrite` (applying a checked symbolic rewrite rule), `:validation` (exhaustive domain evaluation for law, solver, or program certification), `:matching` (structural pattern matching in `:code-match` or propagator library matching), and `:discovery` (observational bucketing, invariant filtering, and guard search).
- **Factorization & orchestration buckets**: `:anti-unification`, `:au-compilation`, `:au-substitution`, `:quotient`, `:partition`, `:reassembly`, `:fiber-clustering`, `:context-frame`, `:heuristic-condensation`, `:view-discovery`, `:locality-scheduling`, `:corpus-encoding`, `:packet-assembly`, and `:construction`.

Individual VM instructions are priced by operand bit size (`value-bit-size`): scalar comparisons and field operations cost $1 + \text{operand bits}$, integer multiplication costs $2 + \mathrm{bits}(a) \cdot \mathrm{bits}(b)$, and `:at`/`:gather`/`:lookup` prepay $1 + \mathrm{bits}(\text{index}) + \mathrm{bits}(\text{length})$ **before** checking bounds or allocating result copies. *(Note: Tariff v9 was introduced in the post-audit refactor when `fold-constants`, `infer-fibers`, `condense-heuristic`, and `synthesize.lisp` were tightened; historical M1–M7 trace numbers in `results.md` were recorded under Tariff v8 while the live test suite runs under Tariff v9.)*

#### Object-Level vs. Meta-Level Objectives
1. **Object-Level Model Objective $J(M)$**: Ranks candidate models $M$ for a fixed frame $\Omega$:
   $$J(M) = S(M) + S\bigl(\varepsilon(M, \Omega)\bigr) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}}\, W_{\mathrm{run}}(M)$$
   - For **exact synthesis, law discovery, and lossless compression** (Operations 1–3), admissibility requires zero residual ($\varepsilon_\omega = 0$ on all $\omega \in \Omega$, so $S(\varepsilon(M, \Omega)) = 0$), reducing the objective to $J_{\mathrm{exact}}(M) = S(M) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}}\, W_{\mathrm{run}}(M)$ (`candidate-objective` in `enumerate.lisp`).
   - For **lossy heuristic condensation** (Operation 4, `start-heuristic-search` / `condense-heuristic` in `synthesize.lisp` and `residual-bit-size` in `factor.lisp`), $S(\varepsilon(\hat{\pi}, F))$ charges zero bits when the residual list is empty and otherwise charges the residual-count header $\mathrm{positive\text{-}bit\text{-}size}(|\varepsilon| + 1)$ plus $\mathrm{positive\text{-}bit\text{-}size}(i + 1) + \mathrm{value\text{-}bit\text{-}size}(w_i, \tau)$ for every row $\omega_i$ where $\hat{\pi}(\mathbf{x}_{\omega_i})$ fails `check-requirement` against the original requirement $B_{\omega_i}(Z)$. During selection $\varepsilon$ is priced against the `lookup-model` baseline, and **at deployment $\varepsilon$ is dropped** because the downstream consumer relies on a 1-in-4 canonical reserve and authoritative verification.
2. **Meta-Level Procedural Objective $J_{\mathrm{procedure}}(P)$**: Evaluates end-to-end discovery procedures $P$ (including search, rewrite preparation, propagation, lifting, view discovery, and recursive decomposition):
   $$J_{\mathrm{procedure}}(P) = J(M_P) + \lambda_{\mathrm{search}}\, W_{\mathrm{search}}(P)$$
   where $W_{\mathrm{search}}(P)$ is the `ledger-total` across all work buckets accumulated to produce $M_P$.

### 1.6 Epistemic Grades and Certificates

Every discovered statement, guard, solver, or rewrite rule carries an explicit epistemic status (`law-proposal-status`):

- `:replayed-observations` — holds on a finite set of empirical or held-out trajectory rows.
- `:conjecture` — holds on all tested sample rows of an infinite domain (`:int`) or an incompletely enumerated finite domain (either due to sample scope or budget `:cutoff`).
- `:vacuous` — guard is false on every row of the tested scope ($\Omega_{\mathrm{support}} = \emptyset$).
- `:refuted` — at least one concrete counterexample row $\omega$ was found where the guard holds and the equation/postcondition fails or errors (`law-proposal-counterexample`).
- `:validated` — exhaustively verified across **all** rows of a finite enumerable domain grid $\Omega = D^\Gamma$ (`schema-enumerable-p` and `frame-covers-domain-p`) with at least one positive support row and zero errors/cutoffs. Only `:validated` laws receive an immutable `law-certificate` (`law-certificate-law-bits`, `law-certificate-domain`, `law-certificate-inputs`, `law-certificate-support-size`), and only `:validated` laws may authorize in-place ($\iff$) downward constraint replacement or unverified syntactic rewriting.

### Key Conceptual Distinctions

- **Symbolic variables (uppercase $X, Y, Z$) vs. bound runtime values (lowercase $\mathbf{x}_\omega, y_\omega, z_\omega$)**: Uppercase $X = (X_0, \dots, X_{m-1})$, $Y$, and $Z$ denote formal AST variable nodes (`(var id type)`). Lowercase $\mathbf{x}_\omega = (x_{\omega,0}, \dots, x_{\omega,m-1})$ denotes the concrete runtime value vector stored in `row-known` on row $\omega \in \Omega$, and lowercase $y_\omega, z_\omega \in \mathcal{V}_\tau$ denote concrete runtime target values. Substituting $X \gets \mathbf{x}_\omega$ into an expression $d(X, Z)$ replaces each input variable $X_r$ with the literal `'x_{\omega,r}`, leaving only $Z$ free.
- **Contravariant Pullback (`pullback-frame`) vs. Right-Inverse (Factorization & Witness Solvers $q$)**: Both operations move "backward" through a forward map, but they act on different mathematical objects and carry different guarantees:
  - **Pullback (exact preimage of a predicate — always exists, never loses solutions)**: Given a parent requirement predicate $A_\omega(Y)$ and a forward decoder $Y = d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)$, pulling $A_\omega$ back along $d$ (`pullback-frame`) substitutes $Y \gets d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)$ and conservatively folds closed constants (`fold-constants`) to produce the child requirement predicate $B_\omega(Z) := \mathrm{FoldConstants}(A_\omega(d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)))$. Set-theoretically, $B_\omega(Z)$ defines the exact **preimage fiber** $d_{\mathbf{x}_\omega}^{-1}(\{y \mid A_\omega(y)\}) = \{z \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C} \mid B_\omega(z) = \mathrm{t}\}$. Pullback requires *no* inverse function to exist: if $d$ is many-to-one ($0 \cdot Z = 0$ or $Z / 2 = 1$), $B_\omega(Z)$ simply accepts multiple $z$'s; if $d$ cannot reach $A_\omega$ ($0 \cdot Z = 1$), $B_\omega(Z)$ accepts none. However, leaving $B_\omega(Z)$ as an uninverted predicate means child search must still evaluate $d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, z)$ for every candidate $z$.
  - **Right-Inverse / Section (constructive selection of a single preimage element or decomposition)**: Given a forward operator $F$, a **right-inverse** is a constructive map $G$ satisfying $F(G(y)) = y$ on a guarded domain:
    1. *In Operation 3 (Algebraic Solver Discovery, M4)*: A learned witness solver $q(K, C)$ is a guarded right-inverse of the binary operator $f(K, \cdot)$: when guard $G(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ holds, $z_\omega = q(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ directly computes **one** concrete witness satisfying $f(k_\omega, z_\omega) = c_\omega$ ($\Leftarrow$). Only when $f(k_\omega, \cdot)$ is also injective ($\implies$, uniqueness `:validated` on $\mathbb{F}_p$) is the right-inverse a full two-sided inverse ($\iff$), allowing the pulled-back predicate $B_\omega(Z)$ to be replaced in-place by `(= Z 'z_w)`. When $f(k_\omega, \cdot)$ aliases multiple inputs to the same output (such as truncating integer division $Z / 2 = 1 \implies \{2, 3\}$), any single-valued right-inverse $q$ can pick only one branch ($Z = 2$) and misses $Z = 3$—which is why tentative propagation must retain the exact pullback $B_\omega(Z)$ as a backup lane (see [3:1 Interleaved Scheduling](#sec-3-to-1-scheduling)).
    2. *In Operation 2 (Factorization, M1/M3B/M5/M6/M7)*: Factoring witnessed objects $\{t_\omega\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$ into a shared template/table $p$ and per-row residuals $\{\theta_\omega\}$ computes a right-inverse of the forward reconstruction operator $\mathrm{Apply}(p, \theta_\omega) = t_\omega$. Because many $(p, \theta_\omega)$ decompositions reconstruct the same $t_\omega$, Operation 2 selects the specific right-inverse that minimizes total description bits $S(p) + \sum_\omega S(\theta_\omega)$.
- **What Can Serve as a Decoder $d(X, Z)$, How Child Predicates $B_\omega(Z)$ Are Created, and Their Dual Role with Respect to Residuals ($\theta_\omega, j_\omega$ vs. $\varepsilon_\omega$)**:
  - **What can serve as a decoder $d(X, Z)$ (`pullback-frame` & `compose-model` in `frame.lisp`, `decomposition` in `synthesize.lisp`)**: A decoder is **not** a restricted primitive or fixed inverse table; it is **any well-typed object-language expression** $d \in \mathcal{L}(\Gamma \cup \{Z{:}\tau_C\} \to \tau_P)$ over the parent's input variables $X = (X_0, \dots, X_{m-1})$ plus a distinguished fresh child variable $Z = \texttt{(var child-id }\tau_C\texttt{)}$ whose return type equals the parent's target type $\tau_P$. Alongside $d$, `pullback-frame` takes a **context-projection map** `bindings` $= \bigl((c_0 . e_0(X)), (c_1 . e_1(X)), \dots\bigr)$ specifying which parent expressions $e_r(X)$ are passed as known runtime inputs $\mathbf{x}^C_\omega = (e_0(\mathbf{x}_\omega), \dots)$ to the recursive child synthesis problem:
    - *Information isolation between child inputs and child checker*: On each row $\omega$, the child synthesizer only sees the projected inputs $\mathbf{x}^C_\omega$, whereas the child requirement predicate $B_\omega(Z) = \mathrm{FoldConstants}(A_\omega(d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)))$ closes over the parent's full observation $\mathbf{x}_\omega$. Once recursive synthesis yields candidate child solutions $e_C(X_C)$, `compose-model` (and `verify-parent-reconstruction` in `synthesize.lisp`) inlines both the context bindings and child candidate back into $d$:
      $$M_{\mathrm{parent}}(X) = d\bigl(X,\, e_C(e_0(X), \dots)\bigr),$$
      producing an ordinary unified AST that is verified and priced at the parent level ($S_{\mathrm{parent}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run,parent}}$), so duplicated context subexpressions pay their full parent description and runtime cost rather than being hidden by a local child optimum.
    - *Four classes of decoders across the milestones*:
      1. **Recursive Top-Down / Outer-Shell Synthesis Targets (M1, M4, M5, M8)**: Any candidate outer expression shell with a subproblem variable $Z : \tau_C$ (or tuple of subproblems $Z : (\texttt{:tuple }\tau_1\,\tau_2)$), such as identity/projection shells $d(X, Z) = Z$ with restricted or synthesized feature bindings `bindings` (used in `dependency-proposals` and `start-dependency-search`), algebraic shells $d(a, b, Z) = (a \cdot a) + Z$, non-injective wrappers $d(X, Z) = Z / 2$, or literal sequence/grid lookups $d(X, Z) = \texttt{(at '}R\; Z\texttt{)}$.
      2. **Conditional & Piecewise View Shells (M7, M8)**: A branch-merging decoder $d(X, Z) = (\texttt{if } G(X)\; Z\; e_{\mathrm{else}}(X))$ or tuple-of-branches decoder $d(X, Z) = (\texttt{if } G(X)\; (\texttt{first } Z)\; (\texttt{second } Z))$ where pulling back $A_\omega(Y)$ folds inactive branch rows to $B_\omega(Z) = \texttt{'t}$ (don't-care) and active rows to $A_\omega(Z)$.
      3. **Anti-Unified Pattern / Syntax Constructors (`compile-au-constructor` in M1, M3B, M4, M6)**: An anti-unified template $p(?_0, \dots, ?_{k-1})$ lowered into a `:code`-typed constructor expression $d(Z)$ over hole variable(s) $Z$, reducing residual syntax generation or law-corpus specialization to ordinary child-frame synthesis.
      4. **Latent Concept & Symbolic Evaluation Wrappers (M3C, M5)**: In M3C, a `:code-eval` rewrite context where $Z : (\texttt{:code }\dots)$ is a candidate rewritten AST; in M5, a generative observation model $d(X, Z)$ (such as modular offset $\iota(t) + Z$) where $Z$ is an unobserved latent concept per row or cluster.
  - **Four mechanisms that create child predicates $B_\omega(Z)$**:
    1. *Contravariant Pullback + Conservative Constant Folding (`pullback-frame` in `frame.lisp` & `fold-constants` in `requirements.lisp`)*: Substitutes $X \gets \text{'}\mathbf{x}_\omega$ into $d(X, Z)$, substitutes $d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)$ for the parent target inside $A_\omega(Y)$, and folds closed ($Z$-free) subtrees and total structural identities in the VM to yield $B_\omega(Z) := \mathrm{FoldConstants}(A_\omega(d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)))$.
    2. *Downward Algebraic Propagation (`propagate-arithmetic-child` in `laws.lisp`)*: Matches an unsimplified arithmetic wrapper inside $B_\omega(Z)$ (such as `(= (+ '4 Z) '1)` or `(= (/ Z '2) '1)`) against the learned `propagator-library` and either replaces $B_\omega(Z)$ in-place with `(= Z 'v_w)` (when certified $\iff$) or schedules `(= Z 'v_w)` on `:primary` (3 quanta) alongside the exact $B_\omega(Z)$ on `:fallback` (1 quantum in `propagation-search`).
    3. *AU Template Lowering (`compile-au-constructor` & `au-substitutions-frame` in `factor.lisp`)*: Projects per-row hole substitutions $\theta_\omega$ from `anti-unify` (or pulls back through the compiled `:code` constructor $d(Z)$) into child predicates $B_\omega(Z)$ over the hole values.
    4. *Bounded Fiber Conjunction (`conjoin-requirements` in `requirements.lisp` & `infer-fibers` in `factor.lisp`)*: Intersects compatible child predicates across rows assigned to the same latent block $\Omega_k$ to form the tightened cluster predicate $B_{c_k}(Z) = \bigwedge_{\omega \in \Omega_k} B_\omega(Z)$.
  - **Dual role of child predicates $B_\omega(Z)$ with respect to residuals ($\theta_\omega, j_\omega$ vs. $\varepsilon_\omega$)**:
    1. *Lossless Factorization Residuals $\theta_\omega \in \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega)$ and Latent Regime Indices $j_\omega$ (Operations 2 & 3)*: In lossless decomposition $t_\omega = \mathrm{Apply}(p, \theta_\omega)$ or $y_\omega \sim d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, z_\omega)$, the child predicate $B_\omega(Z)$ is the **exact indicator of the admissible residual set** $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \{z \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C} \mid B_\omega(z) = \mathrm{t}\}$. When $d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, \cdot)$ is injective, $B_\omega(Z)$ simplifies to `(= Z 'theta_w)`, pinning down a unique residual $\theta_\omega$. When $d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, \cdot)$ is many-to-one ($0 \cdot Z = 0 \implies B_\omega(Z) = \texttt{'t}$, or $Z / 2 = 1 \implies \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \{2, 3\}$), $B_\omega(Z)$ leaves $\theta_\omega$ **underconstrained** rather than prematurely picking one value:
       - On an absorbing/don't-care row where $B_\omega(Z) = \texttt{'t}$ ($\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}$), child synthesis accepts *whatever* value $e_C(\mathbf{x}^C_\omega)$ naturally produces on that row, paying **0 extra description bits** to fit row $\omega$.
       - On multi-witness rows in `infer-fibers` (e.g., $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_1)=\{1,2\}$ and $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_3)=\{1,4\}$), conjoining $B_{\omega_1}(Z) \land B_{\omega_3}(Z)$ narrows the shared cluster residual to `(= Z '1)` with discrete cluster index residual $j_\omega \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ and **zero reconstruction error ($\varepsilon_\omega = 0$)**.
    2. *Lossy Heuristic Exception Residuals $\varepsilon(\hat{\pi}, F)$ (Operation 4, `start-heuristic-search` & `condense-heuristic` in `synthesize.lisp`)*: When `condense-heuristic` compresses an empirical child frame $F = \{(\mathbf{x}_{\omega_i}, B_{\omega_i}(Z))\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$ into a compact intensional program $\hat{\pi}(X)$, it evaluates each candidate's prediction $\hat{z}_{\omega_i} = \hat{\pi}(\mathbf{x}_{\omega_i})$ against row $\omega_i$'s **original** requirement $B_{\omega_i}(\hat{z}_{\omega_i})$ via `check-requirement`:
       - **Hit ($B_{\omega_i}(\hat{z}_{\omega_i}) = \mathrm{t} \implies \varepsilon_{\omega_i} = 0$)**: Any prediction $\hat{z}_{\omega_i} \in \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_i)$—including *any* value when $B_{\omega_i}(Z) = \texttt{'t}$ or *any* member of a multi-valued fiber—satisfies `check-requirement` and costs **0 residual bits**.
       - **Miss ($B_{\omega_i}(\hat{z}_{\omega_i}) = \mathrm{nil}$ or runtime error $\implies \Delta S_{\mathrm{miss}}(i) > 0$)**: Only rows where $B_{\omega_i}(\hat{z}_{\omega_i})$ fails pay into the two-part exception code cost $S(\varepsilon(\hat{\pi}, F))$ during training selection, after which $\varepsilon$ is discarded at deployment.
- **Value-space fiber $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) \subseteq \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}$ vs. row-space partition block $\Omega_k \subseteq \Omega$**: For a single row $\omega \in \Omega$, its **admissible fiber** $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \{z \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C} \mid B_\omega(z) = \mathrm{t}\}$ lives in the target value space $\mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}$. Conversely, a **partition block** (or level set) $\Omega_k = \{\omega \in \Omega \mid j_\omega = k\}$ is a subset of observation rows in $\Omega$. A block $\Omega_k$ is jointly realizable by a single latent concept value $c_k \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}$ if and only if the intersection of its value-space fibers is non-empty:
  $$\bigcap_{\omega \in \Omega_k} \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) \neq \emptyset \iff \exists c_k \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}\;\forall \omega \in \Omega_k,\; B_\omega(c_k) = \mathrm{t}.$$
- **What `pairs` and `anchors` mean (between frames vs. within a frame, and how both are computed)**:
  - **Meaning**: Whereas $A_\omega(Y)$ constrains row $\omega$'s output unary-wise, `pairs` and `anchors` express **relational equivalence and separation constraints across rows of $\Omega$** without fixing concrete target values in advance. Specifically, `pairs` $= \{(\omega_a, \omega_b)\}$ are **intra-block must-agree links** requiring $M(e, \omega_a) = M(e, \omega_b)$ (checked by `classify-observation-bucket` to set `observation-bucket-invariant-p`), while `anchors` $= (\omega_{r_0}, \dots, \omega_{r_{K-1}})$ are **cross-block diversity representatives** requiring $e$ to take at least two distinct values across different blocks (setting `observation-bucket-informative-p` so trivial constants are excluded).
  - **Between frames vs. within a frame**: `pairs` and `anchors` naturally arise when reorienting or factoring one frame into another—for example, `transition-probes` reorients a transition frame $\Omega_{\mathrm{step}}: (a,b) \mapsto (a',b')$ into a $2N$-row probe frame $\Omega_{\mathrm{probe}}$ whose `pairs` `((\omega :before) (\omega :after))` remember which rows came from the same step in $\Omega_{\mathrm{step}}$. Storing `:pairs` and `:anchors` on `frame` (`with-frame-linkage`) allows any newly proposed linkage (from a transition, a Boolean view guard, or fiber clustering) to produce a self-contained `frame` ready for `make-observation-scope`.
  - **Computing `pairs` and `anchors`**: Neither needs to be hand-supplied. Given any partition $\Omega = \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{K-1} \Omega_k$ (from `partition-rows`, a Milestone 7 guard split `evaluate-guard-partition`, or Milestone 5 `infer-fibers`), `partition-pairs-and-anchors` (`frame.lisp`) and `fiber-pairs-and-anchors` (`factor.lisp`) automatically compute:
    1. `pairs`: for each block $\Omega_k = (\omega_{k,0}, \omega_{k,1}, \dots, \omega_{k,m-1})$ with $m \ge 2$, the $m - 1$ adjacent chain links $\bigl((\omega_{k,0}, \omega_{k,1}), (\omega_{k,1}, \omega_{k,2}), \dots\bigr)$ enforcing constancy across the entire block $\Omega_k$ in $O(m)$ checks; and
    2. `anchors`: the leading representative $\omega_{k,0}$ of each multi-row block $\Omega_k$ (when $K \ge 2$), enforcing separation across blocks.
- **Extensional (explicit lookup table) vs. Intensional (generative symbolic program) descriptions**: An **extensional** model (`lookup-model`, `quotient-model`) stores an explicit per-row table of target values or regime indices $j_\omega$, so its description length $S$ grows linearly with $|\Omega|$ and it cannot evaluate inputs outside $\Omega$. An **intensional** model (`program-model`, or a condensed selector $\hat{\pi}(X)$ from `condense-heuristic` / `start-selector-search`) computes the target or regime index via a symbolic expression over $X$, paying a fixed description bit cost $S$ independent of $|\Omega|$.
- **Branch-Selection Policy, 3:1 Interleaved Scheduling & the 1-in-4 Backup Reserve (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`)**: Whenever a search procedure explores multiple decomposition branches (`synthesis-node`s in `start-synthesis` / `start-dependency-search`), tentative propagation lanes (`propagation-search` in M4/M5), locality-ordered occurrence pairs (`schedule-occurrence-pairs` in M7), or library descriptors (M9), a **search policy** must decide which branch to step next. In the current Lisp implementation, this policy is **implicit and deterministic**: active branches are stepped in round-robin order under a 4-turn cycle (`turn = 0, 1, 2, 3, ...`) where turns `0, 1, 2` (**3 work quanta**) advance the **primary shortcut/decomposition queue**, and turn `3` (**1 work quantum**) advances the **canonical backup lane** (plain exhaustive search on the un-narrowed root or pullback frame), with every candidate re-verified against the root specification (see [Section 2.3.4](#sec-3-to-1-scheduling)). Learning an explicit symbolic branch-ordering policy from search traces is the [M8D](#sec-m8) extension.
- **Ambient domain $D$ vs. exhaustive input grid $D^\Gamma$**: $D \in \{\texttt{:int}, \texttt{(:gf } p\texttt{)}\}$ is the ambient numeric sort of the schema, whereas $D^\Gamma = \mathcal{V}_{\tau_0} \times \dots \times \mathcal{V}_{\tau_{m-1}}$ is the Cartesian product of the input variable domains (which may mix scalars, Booleans, tuples, sequences, or `:code` syntax).
- **Object-level model objective $J(M)$, meta-level procedural objective $J_{\mathrm{procedure}}(P)$, and "mountain passes"**: Candidate synthesis and compression rank final assembled models $M$ by $J(M) = S(M) + S(\varepsilon(M, \Omega)) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(M)$ (with $\varepsilon \equiv 0$ for exact models and priced residual $\varepsilon$ during lossy heuristic condensation), while search control, propagation, and rewrite amortization evaluate whole discovery procedures $P$ by $J_{\mathrm{procedure}}(P) = J(M_P) + \lambda_{\mathrm{search}} W_{\mathrm{search}}(P)$. Because intermediate transformations (such as `pullback-frame`, `orient-arithmetic-requirements`, `evaluate-guard-partition`, or `apply-checked-rewrite`) may temporarily expand representation size $S$ or pay upfront work $W$ to simplify downstream computation, work accounting is distributed across local steps in `ledger`, all child candidates are retained (`search-job-proposals`), and $J(M)$ is evaluated on the reassembled parent expression (`compose-model`, `verify-parent-reconstruction`, `price-view-combinations`).

### 2.1 Operation 1: Observational Conservation Across Frame Rows

As the enumerator generates well-typed expressions $e \in \mathcal{L}(\Gamma)$ in canonical order of increasing description length $S(e)$, the resumable VM evaluates the **Outcome Matrix**:
$$M(e, \omega) = \mathrm{Eval}(e, \mathbf{x}_\omega) \in \mathcal{V}_\tau \cup \{\bot_{\mathrm{err}}, \bot_{\mathrm{cutoff}}\}.$$
Four milestone capabilities are direct instances of conservation laws on $M(e, \omega)$:

1. **Per-Row Requirement Satisfaction (M1 Core Synthesis)**:
   A single expression $e$ is a valid program for frame $\Omega$ if and only if it satisfies the unary conservation predicate across all rows with zero residual ($\varepsilon_\omega = 0$):
   $$\forall \omega \in \Omega,\quad M(e, \omega) \notin \{\bot_{\mathrm{err}}, \bot_{\mathrm{cutoff}}\} \;\land\; \mathrm{Eval}\bigl(A_\omega(Y),\, [Y \gets M(e, \omega)]\bigr) = \mathrm{t}.$$
   Among all satisfying expressions, search (`search-job` in `enumerate.lisp`) selects $e$ minimizing $J_{\mathrm{exact}}(e) = S(e) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(e)$.

2. **Cross-Row Observational Equivalence & Action Invariance (M2)**:
   - **Empirical Equivalence (`observation-bucket`)**: Two expressions $e_1, e_2$ belong to the same observational equivalence class on $\Omega$ (`observed-agreement-p`) if and only if they are total on $\Omega$ and produce identical row vectors:
     $$e_1 \sim_\Omega e_2 \iff \forall \omega \in \Omega,\quad M(e_1, \omega) = M(e_2, \omega) \notin \{\bot_{\mathrm{err}}, \bot_{\mathrm{cutoff}}\}.$$
     Note that erroring expressions ($M(e, \omega) = \bot_{\mathrm{err}}$) never enter an equivalence bucket and never count as agreeing (`tests/observations.lisp`). When the frame grows from $\Omega$ to $\Omega' \supset \Omega$, equivalence buckets refine monotonically by partitioning each bucket on the new columns $\Omega' \setminus \Omega$ while preserving the original scope's snapshot unchanged.
   - **Action Invariance (`observation-invariants`)**: Given a state transition or symmetry action $T$ represented by paired before/after rows $(\omega, T\omega)$ (`pairs`) and cross-orbit reference rows (`anchors`), an expression $e$ is an **informative invariant** if and only if it is conserved along every trajectory step while varying across distinct orbits:
     $$\bigl(\forall (\omega, T\omega) \in \mathrm{pairs},\; M(e, \omega) = M(e, T\omega)\bigr) \;\land\; \bigl(\exists (\omega_1, \omega_2) \in \mathrm{anchors},\; M(e, \omega_1) \neq M(e, \omega_2)\bigr).$$

3. **Exhaustive Finite-Domain Law Certification (M3A)**:
   When the frame covers the entire finite input grid $\Omega = D^\Gamma$ (`schema-enumerable-p` and `frame-covers-domain-p`), empirical equivalence $e_1 \sim_{D^\Gamma} e_2$ is promoted by `start-law-validation` / `resume-law-validation` (`laws.lisp`) from `:conjecture` to `:validated`, producing an immutable `law-certificate`. On infinite domains ($D = \texttt{:int}$) or proper subsets $\Omega \subsetneq D^\Gamma$, survival across $\Omega$ remains graded as `:conjecture`.

4. **Support Masks & Exact Guard Synthesis (M3A)**:
   When a candidate equation $e_1 = e_2$ (or a partial expression $e$ inside an observation job with `:retain-outcomes t`) succeeds on a non-empty proper subset of rows $\Omega_{\mathrm{ok}} \subsetneq \Omega$ and fails or errors (`:error`) on $\Omega \setminus \Omega_{\mathrm{ok}}$, `observed-support` and `support-frame` (`laws.lisp`) project the row outcomes onto a Boolean indicator frame $\Omega_{\mathrm{guard}}$ with target requirement:
   $$A^{\mathrm{guard}}_\omega(G) \equiv \bigl(G = \mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{ok}}}(\omega)\bigr).$$
   Running standard synthesis (Op 1.1) on $\Omega_{\mathrm{guard}}$ with **zero residual ($\varepsilon_\omega = 0$)** synthesizes the exact domain-validity guard $G(X)$ (`propose-guard-problems`, `start-guard-search`), yielding the guarded law $G(X) \implies e_1(X) = e_2(X)$ (`propose-guarded-laws`).

### 2.2 Operation 2: Factorization & Latent Fiber Proposals ($S$ Compression)

Factorization reduces total description length $S$ by splitting a collection of witnessed objects $\{t_\omega\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$ into a **shared reusable structure $p$** (paid once in the model header/template) and **per-instance residuals $\{\theta_\omega\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$** such that a deterministic reconstruction operator `Apply` recovers each target exactly:
$$\mathrm{Apply}(p, \theta_\omega) = t_\omega \quad (\forall \omega \in \Omega), \qquad S(p) + \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} S(\theta_\omega) < \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} S(t_\omega).$$
Operation 2 instantiates `Apply` across three algebras:

1. **Term Algebra ($\mathrm{Apply} = \mathrm{Subst}$ — M1, M3B, M4, M6)**:
   - **Aligned Anti-Unification (`anti-unify` in `factor.lisp`)**: Given typed S-expressions $t_1, \dots, t_N$ (which may be object-language programs, quoted `:code` laws in M3B/M4, or data trees in M6), `anti-unify` computes the least general generalization (LGG) template $p$ containing typed value holes `(hole i type :kind :value)` (`#:?0, #:?1, ...`) and operator holes `(hole i sig :kind :fn)` (when applications share arity and argument/return signature `(arg-types . return-type)`), together with per-row substitution bindings $\theta_\omega$. Repeated subterms across all rows are bound to the **same hole ID** via a shared residual-signature table (`au-state`), enforcing structural equality constraints such as `(?op ?x ?x)` on `(+ '2 '2)` and `(* '3 '3)`. Calling `compile-au-constructor` lowers $p$ into a first-class typed constructor expression so `au-substitutions-frame` reduces residual synthesis to standard child-frame search.
   - **Unaligned Subgraph Correspondence Search (`discover-subexpression-abstractions` in `factor.lisp` — M6)**: When a shared pattern $p$ occurs at **varying paths** $\pi_\omega$ inside larger trees $T_\omega = W_\omega\bigl[\pi_\omega \gets \mathrm{Subst}(p, \theta_\omega)\bigr]$, `collect-tree-occurrences` indexes all non-leaf subterms, seeds candidate patterns by anti-unifying pairs of compatible subtrees, and greedily extends occurrence sets across the remaining trees to maximize `occurrence-saved-bits` under the spliced pre-order packet codec (`corpus-abstraction-model`).

2. **Sequence & Table Algebra ($\mathrm{Apply} = \mathrm{Gather}$, $\mathrm{At}$, or $\mathrm{Reassemble}$ — M1, M7)**:
   - **Quotient View (`quotient-values`, `restore-quotient`, `quotient-model`)**: Factors a value sequence $\{t_\omega\}_{\omega=0}^{N-1}$ (over scalars or generated tuple spaces) into a deduplicated representative table $R = (r_0, \dots, r_{K-1})$ and an index sequence $j_\omega \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ such that $t_\omega = \mathrm{gather}(R, j_\omega)$ in batch mode (`residual` a list of indices) or $t_\omega = \texttt{(at '}R\; j(X_\omega)\texttt{)}$ in per-row functional mode (`residual` an `:int` AST expression).
   - **Partition View (`partition-rows`, `reassemble-partition`, `partition-model`)**: Factors an observation frame $\Omega$ into regime sub-frames $\Omega_0, \dots, \Omega_{K-1}$ while retaining explicit occurrence coordinates so `reassemble-partition` reconstructs the exact original ordered sequence with ties and multiplicity.

3. **Pulled-Back Requirement Algebra ($\mathrm{Apply} = \text{Bounded Fiber Intersection Proposal}$ — M5)**:
   When a parent requirement $A_\omega(Y)$ is pulled back through a decoder $Y = d(X, Z)$ to yield per-row child requirements $B_\omega(Z)$ on $\omega \in \Omega$, each row $\omega$ defines an **admissible fiber** in the child target space $\mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}$:
   $$\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \{z \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C} \mid B_\omega(z) = \mathrm{t}\}.$$
   Unsupervised concept lifting (`infer-fibers` in `factor.lisp`) proposes a partition of the row set $\Omega = \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{K-1} \Omega_k$ (via latent row labels $j_\omega \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$) and a shared concept requirement $B_{c_k}(Z)$ for each block $\Omega_k$ such that:
   $$B_{c_k}(Z) \equiv \bigwedge_{\omega \in \Omega_k} B_\omega(Z) \neq \texttt{'nil} \iff \bigcap_{\omega \in \Omega_k} \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) \neq \emptyset.$$
   Because fiber intersection is non-associative with respect to greedy merging (for example, on the 4-row bridge-vs-leaf configuration $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_1)=\{1,2\}$, $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_2)=\{2,3\}$, $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_3)=\{1,4\}$, $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_4)=\{3,5\}$, greedily merging $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ on the bridge value $Z=2$ strands $\omega_3$ and $\omega_4$ into $K=3$ clusters, whereas the global optimum is $K=2$ with $\{\omega_1,\omega_3\} \mapsto Z=1$ and $\{\omega_2,\omega_4\} \mapsto Z=3$), `infer-fibers` executes a **bounded combinatorial partition search** under a lexicographic proxy objective:
   - **Stage A (Exact Same-Input Determinism)**: `tighten-same-input-requirements` (`factor.lisp`) intersects requirements **only** across rows sharing identical complete `row-known` tuples $\mathbf{x}_\omega$ (including `nil` for 0-input problems) and preserves `'nil` on conflicting same-input rows so contradictions are never silently dropped. Coordinate subset selection is not guessed inside `tighten-same-input-requirements`; instead, coordinate projections and synthesized feature selectors are evaluated as explicit competing decomposition hypotheses by `start-dependency-search` (`synthesize.lisp`).
   - **Stage B (Bounded Branch-and-Bound Lexicographic Partition Proposal)**: Under `with-work-limit` (`:budget`) and `:branch-limit`, `infer-fibers` deduplicates satisfiable rows with identical requirements into unique groups $G_0, \dots, G_{U-1}$ (ordered with constrained groups before unconstrained `'t` groups, then by ascending requirement `body-size`), seeds a valid singleton-group fallback before recursion (so `branch-limit = 0` or immediate budget exhaustion still returns a well-formed fallback partition), and searches over compatible group partitions using `conjoin-requirements` to minimize cluster count $K$, breaking ties by minimum repeated requirement `body-size` bits $\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} |\Omega_k|\, S_{\mathrm{body}}(B_{c_k})$ and minimum empirical label entropy (maximizing $\sum_k |\Omega_k|^2$).
   - **Stage C (Computed Linkages & Explicit Uncertainty Envelope)**: From the proposed partition $\Omega = \bigsqcup_{k=0}^{K-1} \Omega_k$, `infer-fibers` calls `partition-pairs-and-anchors` to attach intra-fiber must-agree `pairs` and cross-fiber must-vary `anchors` onto `refined-frame`, and returns `(values classes labels refined-frame conflicts status)` where the 5th value `status` is a `message` reporting `:claim :partition-proposal` (or `:claim :cutoff` if truncated by `budget` or `branch-limit`) and `:feasibility :unresolved` (since downstream selector/child synthesis must still verify whether the proposed partition leads to a compact realizable parent model).

### 2.3 Operation 3: Causal Reorientation, Contravariant Pullback, Conservative Constant Folding & Dual-Grade Propagation

Whereas forward evaluation pushes known inputs $\mathbf{x}_\omega$ forward through an expression $e$ to produce outputs, hierarchical synthesis and algebraic reasoning move **contravariantly** from parent requirements to child subproblems:

#### 2.3.1 Causal Reorientation (`orient-observations` in `frame.lisp`, `orient-arithmetic-requirements` in `laws.lisp` — M1, M2, M4)
Given recorded relational observations $(v_{\omega,0}, \dots, v_{\omega,k-1})$ (stored in `observation-scope` or forward execution tables), reorientation selects any subset of coordinates as the new known input vector $\mathbf{x}'_\omega$ and projects the remaining coordinates into the new target requirement $A'_\omega(Y)$—charging `:orientation` work and **zero `:simulation` work**. When multiple recorded tuples share the same projected input $\mathbf{x}'_\omega$ (as in non-injective operations like $0 \cdot Z = 0$ or integer division), `orient-arithmetic-requirements` preserves the exact relational predicate $R_f(K, Z, C)$ rather than collapsing or dropping witnesses.

#### 2.3.2 Contravariant Pullback & Conservative Constant Folding (`pullback-frame` & `compose-model` in `frame.lisp`, `fold-constants` in `requirements.lisp` — M1, M3C, M4, M5, M7, M8)
Given a parent frame $\Omega_P$ with row requirements $A_\omega(Y)$ ($Y : \tau_P$), a **candidate outer decoder** $d(X, Z) \in \mathcal{L}(\Gamma \cup \{Z{:}\tau_C\} \to \tau_P)$ (any well-typed object-language expression over the parent's inputs $X$ and a fresh child variable $Z = \texttt{(var child-id }\tau_C\texttt{)}$ whose return type equals $\tau_P$), and a **child context-projection alist** `bindings` $= \bigl((c_0 . e_0(X)), \dots, (c_{k-1} . e_{k-1}(X))\bigr)$, `(pullback-frame parent decoder child-id child-type bindings)` constructs the child frame $\Omega_C$:

- **Child inputs $\mathbf{x}^C_\omega$**: Evaluates each parent context expression $e_r(\mathbf{x}_\omega)$ in the VM to form the child row's known input vector $\mathbf{x}^C_\omega = \bigl(e_0(\mathbf{x}_\omega), \dots, e_{k-1}(\mathbf{x}_\omega)\bigr)$.
- **Child requirement predicate $B_\omega(Z)$**: Substitutes the parent row's known input values $X \gets \text{'}\mathbf{x}_\omega$ and maps `child-id` $\mapsto Z = \texttt{(var 0 }\tau_C\texttt{)}$ inside $d(X, Z)$, substitutes the closed decoder $d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)$ for `X0` in $A_\omega(\texttt{X0})$, and simplifies the resulting unary predicate via `fold-constants` (`requirements.lisp`):
$$B_\omega(Z) := \mathrm{FoldConstants}\Bigl(A_\omega\bigl(d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z)\bigr)\Bigr).$$
Specifically, `fold-constants` enforces a **strictly conservative, partiality-preserving simplification contract**:

1. **Single Lexical Budget**: Uses one shared `remaining` work counter across both recursive AST walk steps and closed-subtree VM evaluations (`start-evaluation` / `advance-evaluation`); when called with `:budget 0`, it returns the original expression immediately with `0` ledger charges.
2. **Closed-Subtree Evaluation with Error/Cutoff Preservation**: Evaluates closed ($Z$-free) subtrees in the metered VM (charging `:orientation` work) and replaces them with `(lit type value)` only when evaluation completes with `:value`; any subtree that ends in `:error` or `:cutoff` is left intact so runtime failure behavior and remaining budget are preserved.
3. **Totality-Gated Structural Identities**: Simplifies `(if 't a b) -> a` and `(if 'nil a b) -> b`, and requires `provably-total-p` before applying branch idempotence `(if c x x) -> x`, reflexivity `(= e e) -> 't`, `:member` normalization (`(member x '()) -> 'nil`, `(member x '(v)) -> (= x 'v)`), tuple equality decomposition, or `:at` on `:tuple` $\beta$-reduction:
   $$\bigl(\texttt{= (tuple } e_1 \dots e_m\texttt{) '}(v_1 \dots v_m)\bigr) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{provably\text{-}total\text{-}p}} \bigwedge_{i=1}^m (\texttt{= } e_i\;\texttt{'}v_i), \qquad \texttt{(at (tuple } e_0 \dots e_{m-1}\texttt{) '}i\texttt{)} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{provably\text{-}total\text{-}p}} e_i.$$
   In particular, `(if c 'nil 'nil)` is folded to `'nil` **only** when `c` is `provably-total-p`, so partial conditions such as `(= (at '(5 6) X0) '5)` or `(= (/ '1 X0) '0)` inside `bool-not`, `bool-and`, or `:if` never turn a runtime `:error` into `'nil` or `'t`.
4. **Conjunction Equality Propagation, `:member` Filtering & Tuple Coordinate Reassembly**: Inside conjunctions `(if cond yes 'nil)`, `simplify-conjunction` propagates total equalities `(= term 'v)` into sibling conjuncts, filters finite `:member` domains `(member term '(v_1 ... v_k))` against other conjuncts that mention `term` (dropping only those conjuncts entailed across all surviving domain values while preserving unrelated sibling conjuncts such as constraints on another tuple coordinate), and reassembles complete coordinate equalities $\bigwedge_{i=0}^{m-1} (\texttt{= (at } Z\;\texttt{'}i\texttt{) '}v_i)$ back into canonical tuple equality `(= Z '(v_0 ... v_{m-1}))`.
5. **No Built-In Host Table Inversion**: `fold-constants` does **not** invert 1D `(at 'R Z)` or 2D `(at (at 'G (at Z '0)) (at Z '1))` lookup tables in the host; pulled-back lookup requirements remain ordinary symbolic predicates (e.g., `(= (at '(8 2 9) X0) '2)`) whose witnesses are synthesized by ordinary search.
- **Re-inlining the solved child (`compose-model`)**: Once synthesis finds a child expression $e_C(X_C)$ satisfying $B_\omega(e_C(\mathbf{x}^C_\omega)) = \mathrm{t}$ across $\Omega_C$, `(compose-model decoder child-id e_C bindings)` substitutes $c_r \gets e_r(X)$ into $e_C$ and substitutes the resulting expression for `child-id` inside $d(X, Z)$ to produce the unified parent AST $d\bigl(X, e_C(e_0(X), \dots)\bigr)$.

#### 2.3.3 Immediate Structural & Cross-Row Conflict Detection (`frame-conflict-p` in `frame.lisp` — M5, M7, M8C)
Immediately after `pullback-frame` and `fold-constants` (before any child candidate enumeration begins), `frame-conflict-p` inspects the pulled-back child frame $\Omega_C$:

- **Row-Level Structural Conflict (`:unsatisfiable-row`)**: If any row $\omega \in \Omega_C$ folds to `B_\omega(Z) = 'nil` (for example, pulling `(= Y '(2 3))` back through the diagonal decoder $d(Z) = \texttt{(tuple } Z\; Z\texttt{)}$ folds via tuple injectivity and equality propagation to `'nil`), the decoder $d$ is rejected in $O(1)$ work.
- **Cross-Row Context/Partition Conflict (`:incompatible-rows`)**: Grouping rows by identical child input vector $\mathbf{x}_\omega$ (`row-known`), if two rows $\omega_1, \omega_2$ share the same child inputs $\mathbf{x}_{\omega_1} = \mathbf{x}_{\omega_2}$ (e.g., because a candidate view split or restricted child signature dropped a distinguishing coordinate $r$) and their requirements conjoin via `conjoin-requirements` to `'nil` ($\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_1) \cap \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_2) = \emptyset$), `frame-conflict-p` returns `(values t :incompatible-rows keys)` and prunes the invalid split or decomposition immediately.

#### 2.3.4 Dual-Grade Downward Algebraic Propagation & 3:1 Interleaved Scheduling (`propagate-arithmetic-child` & `propagation-search` in `laws.lisp` — M4, M5, M7, M8) {#sec-3-to-1-scheduling}
When $B_\omega(Z)$ still contains an unsimplified operator application `(= (op K Z) 'c)` or `(= (op Z K) 'c)` (with known constant $K = k_\omega$ after `fold-constants`), the structural `:code-*` matcher (`compile-propagator-matcher`, `match-arithmetic-propagator`) matches the decoder against the learned `propagator-library`. Each `arithmetic-solver` in the library is synthesized by `discover-arithmetic-solver` and graded by `validate-arithmetic-solver` via two separate implications over $(K, C, Z)$:

- **Witness Law ($\Leftarrow$, Right-Inverse)**: $G(K, C) \implies R_f\bigl(K,\, q(K, C),\, C\bigr)$ (does $z_\omega = q(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ satisfy the forward relation when guard $G(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ holds?).
- **Uniqueness Law ($\implies$, Injectivity)**: $\bigl(G(K, C) \land R_f(K, Z, C)\bigr) \implies Z = q(K, C)$ (is $q(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ the **only** solution in $\mathcal{V}_{\tau_C}$?).

Downward propagation then acts according to the solver's epistemic grade:

- **Grade 1 — Certified Equivalence ($\iff$, e.g., $\mathbb{F}_p$ addition, subtraction, and nonzero multiplication/division)**: Both the witness law ($\Leftarrow$) and uniqueness law ($\implies$) have status `:validated` across the full finite domain $D^\Gamma$. Whenever $G(k_\omega, c_\omega) = \mathrm{t}$ and $q(k_\omega, c_\omega) = v_\omega$ passes forward verification $B_\omega(v_\omega) = \mathrm{t}$, `propagate-arithmetic-child` replaces $B_\omega(Z)$ **in-place** in the primary child frame with the direct equality `(= Z 'v_w)` (`propagation-certified-p = t`, `propagation-fallback = nil`). On rows where $G(k_\omega, c_\omega) = \mathrm{nil}$ (such as $0 \cdot Z = 0$, where $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \mathbb{F}_p$, or $0 \cdot Z = 1$, where $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \emptyset$), $B_\omega(Z)$ is left untouched as an exact predicate.
- **Grade 2 — Tentative Witness & 3:1 Scheduled Propagation ($\Leftarrow$, e.g., $\mathbb{Z}$ arithmetic where sample validation yields `:conjecture`, or truncating integer division where uniqueness is `:refuted` because $Z / 2 = 1$ admits $\{2, 3\}$)**:
  - **Why in-place replacement is unsafe for $\Leftarrow$**: Because $q(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ is only a right-inverse (it picks *one* witness $v_\omega$ out of a potentially multi-valued fiber $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega)$) or was only tested on a finite sample of $\mathbb{Z}$, replacing $B_\omega(Z)$ in-place with `(= Z 'v_w)` can over-constrain the child problem. For example, if row 1 has $Z / 2 = 1$ ($\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_1) = \{2, 3\}$, solver picks $v_1 = 2$) and row 2 on the same input has $Z / 3 = 1$ ($\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_2) = \{3, 4, 5\}$, solver picks $v_2 = 3$), replacing both rows in-place demands $Z = 2$ and $Z = 3$ simultaneously, making the child frame unsatisfiable even though $Z = 3$ satisfies both rows.
  - **How 3:1 Scheduled Propagation works (`propagation-search`)**: When $G(k_\omega, c_\omega) = \mathrm{t}$ and the proposed value $v_\omega = q(k_\omega, c_\omega)$ passes row-level forward verification $B_\omega(v_\omega) = \mathrm{t}$, `propagate-arithmetic-child` builds **two** child search frames:
    1. A **primary (shortcut) frame** (`propagation-frame`, lane `:primary`) in which row $\omega$'s requirement is narrowed to the fast singleton equality `(= Z 'v_w)`; and
    2. A **fallback (exact backup) frame** (`propagation-fallback`, lane `:fallback`) retaining the exact, un-narrowed pulled-back requirement $B_\omega(Z)$.
    `propagation-search` then steps the two child search jobs in a deterministic **3:1 round-robin cycle** (`turn = 0, 1, 2, 3, ...`): turns `0, 1, 2` (**3 work quanta**) advance `:primary`, and turn `3` (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`, **1 work quantum**) advances `:fallback`. Whenever `:primary` proposes a candidate child expression $e_C$, `propagation-search` immediately re-verifies $e_C$ against the original un-narrowed frame `propagation-original` before accepting it. Moreover, in view discovery (`synthesize.lisp`) and recursive synthesis (`synthesis-node`), if the narrowed `propagation-frame` triggers a same-input conflict (`frame-conflict-p`) on a tentative branch while `propagation-fallback` is conflict-free, search automatically falls back to `propagation-fallback` instead of falsely rejecting the branch.

### 2.4 Operation 4: Lossy Heuristic Condensation, Dependency Synthesis & Multi-Lane Search Control ($W$ Compression)

Operations 1–3 require lossless exactness ($\varepsilon_\omega = 0$) because an unverified error in an object-level program or law corrupts correctness. By contrast, **control heuristics and decomposition proposals**—which choose which partition branch or context dependency to try, which occurrence pair or search continuation to expand next, or which library function to test first—cannot corrupt soundness provided every candidate solution is still checked by the authoritative root verifier and a canonical reserve lane remains active.

`start-heuristic-search` / `resume-heuristic-search` (wrapped by `condense-heuristic` in `synthesize.lisp`) formulates heuristic learning as **resumable, budget-accounted lossy MDL table condensation**:

- Given a finite empirical frame $F = \{(\mathbf{x}_{\omega_i}, B_{\omega_i}(Z))\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$ mapping context features $\mathbf{x}_{\omega_i}$ to child requirement predicates $B_{\omega_i}(Z)$ (with canonical fallback witnesses $w_i \in \mathrm{Fiber}(\omega_i)$ extracted by `row-target-witness`), `heuristic-search` interleaves candidate observation (`observation-job`), requirement checking (`check-requirement`), and residual pricing on the **original** frame requirements under one cumulative `ledger`.
- During training, each completed candidate $\hat{\pi} \in \mathcal{L}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{control}})$ is scored by the full two-part code length plus runtime work (`residual-bit-size` in `factor.lisp`):
  $$J_{\mathrm{heuristic}}(\hat{\pi}) = S(\hat{\pi}) + \underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{|\varepsilon|>0}\,\mathrm{positive\text{-}bit\text{-}size}(|\varepsilon| + 1) + \sum_{i:\,B_{\omega_i}(\hat{\pi}(\mathbf{x}_{\omega_i})) \neq \mathrm{t}} \Bigl(\mathrm{positive\text{-}bit\text{-}size}(i + 1) + \mathrm{value\text{-}bit\text{-}size}(w_i, \tau)\Bigr)}_{S(\varepsilon(\hat{\pi}, F))} + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}}\, W_{\mathrm{run}}(\hat{\pi}).$$
- **Dropping $\varepsilon$ at deployment & preserving `:cutoff`**: Once the minimizing expression $\hat{\pi}^*$ is selected (improving over the uncondensed `lookup-model` baseline when allowed), the exception table $\varepsilon(\hat{\pi}^*, F)$ is **discarded** and `condense-heuristic` returns `(values best-model status-message)` (reporting `:cutoff` if budget expires mid-search). At deployment, only the compact symbolic program $\hat{\pi}^*(X)$ is evaluated in the metered VM, paired with the **1-in-4 canonical reserve schedule** (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`).

Operation 4 powers four control and decomposition loops across the milestones:

1. **Intensional Partition Selectors (`start-selector-search` & `condense-heuristic` — M5, M7)**: After `infer-fibers` (M5) or `discover-partition-views` (M7) proposes latent regime labels $j_\omega \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$, `start-selector-search` / `condense-heuristic` synthesizes an intensional selector program $\hat{\pi}(X)$ deployed at runtime without carrying the per-row lookup table.
2. **Explicit Dependency & Feature Selection (`dependency-proposals` & `start-dependency-search` — M5, M8)**: Rather than hard-coding coordinate selection inside preprocessing, `dependency-proposals` and `start-dependency-search` (`synthesize.lisp`) treat empty-input constants, single-coordinate projections $X_r$, caller-supplied feature bindings, and bounded observation-discovered feature expressions $e(X)$ (such as `(+ X0 X1)`) as explicit child decomposition hypotheses that compete directly against full-input synthesis under the parent objective $J(M_{\mathrm{parent}})$.
3. **Locality & Continuation Return Predictors (M7, M8D)**: In M7 (`schedule-occurrence-pairs`), subtree occurrence pairs are prioritized by structural distance (`compute-tree-locality-positions`) or an induced `:locality-key` expression with a 1-in-4 canonical reserve. In M8D, logged episode continuation returns $G_t = B_t - B_T - \lambda_{\mathrm{search}}(W_T - W_t)$ are condensed via `condense-heuristic` into a symbolic value predictor $\hat{\pi}(\text{features})$ that prioritizes search continuations 3:1 over canonical enumeration.
4. **Compositional Library Descriptors (M9)**: Given a library of opaque function symbols (`F_0, F_1, ...`) accumulated across M1–M8, `condense-heuristic` synthesizes compact symbolic probe descriptors that rank which library primitives to test first on a new partial I/O frame.

### Cross-Cutting Kernel Invariants

1. **The Single-Variable Requirement Convention (`(var 0 target-type)`)**: Regardless of how many inputs $(X_0, \dots, X_{m-1})$ a frame's schema has, `row-requirement` $A_\omega(Y)$ is always a closed Boolean expression over a single free variable `(var 0 target-type)` representing the candidate output $Y$. During `pullback-frame`, the parent's inputs $X$ are substituted with the row's known values $\mathbf{x}_\omega$, and the fresh child variable `child-id` is remapped to `(var 0 child-type)`.
2. **The `validated-program` Execution Boundary (`vm.lisp`)**: Whenever a synthesized AST is promoted into a reusable rewrite transformer (`prepare-rewrite-library`), library operator, or standalone executable artifact, `start-program-validation` / `prepare-program` walks the AST to verify well-typedness, variable scope, and literal validity, charging `:validation` work and wrapping the AST in an immutable `validated-program` executed via `start-validated-evaluation`.
3. **Homoiconic Matcher State (`*last-code-match*` in `vm.lisp`)**: Inside `:code-*` rule and propagator execution, `:code-match` stores the association list of matched hole bindings in the dynamic variable `*last-code-match*` for the duration of the current VM step so subsequent `:code-bind` and `:code-subst` instructions can inspect or instantiate matched subtrees without host closures.

### 3.1 Concrete Example 1: Homoiconic S-Expressions and $O(1)$ Prefix-Free Sizing

In `ast.lisp`, an expression such as $X_0 + (X_1 + X_1)$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$ is constructed via:
```lisp
(let* ((f5 (gf 5))
       (x0 (var 0 f5))
       (x1 (var 1 f5))
       (plus (op :+ f5))
       (expr (app plus x0 (app plus x1 x1))))
  ...)
```
At runtime, `expr` is the ordinary cons tree `(#:+ #:X0 (#:+ #:X1 #:X1))`, and literals such as `(lit f5 2)` are 2-element lists `(#:QUOTE 2)` printed as `'2`. Simultaneously, `app` attaches an `ast-meta` entry in `*node-meta*` recording `:type (:gf 5)`, `:non-var-bits 18`, `:var-count 3`, `:syntax-work 5`, `:total-p t`, and `:free-vars (0 1)`. On a 2-input schema $\Gamma$ ($\mathrm{integer\text{-}length}(2 - 1) = 1$ bit per variable reference), `body-size` computes $18 + 3 \times 1 = 21$ bits in $O(1)$ after validating variable scope. When `expr` is wrapped as a quoted syntax value `(lit (code-type inputs f5) expr)` for M3B law compression, M3C symbolic rewriting, or M4 propagator matching, the exact same cons tree is passed as a first-class value of type `(:code ...)` with zero serialization overhead.

### 3.2 Concrete Example 2: Pullback, Conservative `fold-constants`, `frame-conflict-p`, and $\iff$ vs. $\Leftarrow$ Propagation

Consider a parent row $\omega$ with bound inputs $\mathbf{x}_\omega = (a_\omega, b_\omega) = (2, 3)$ and target requirement $A_\omega(Y) \equiv (\texttt{= } Y\;\texttt{'1})$. Suppose the parent proposes the outer decoder $Y = d(X_0, X_1, Z) = (X_0 + X_0) + Z$.

1. **Pullback + Conservative Constant Folding (`pullback-frame` & `fold-constants`)**: Substituting $X_0 \gets \texttt{'2}$ into $A_\omega(d(X, Z))$ yields `(= (+ (+ '2 '2) Z) '1)`. Because `(+ '2 '2)` contains no occurrence of the child target $Z = \texttt{(var 0 ...)}$, `fold-constants` evaluates `(+ '2 '2)` once in the VM (producing `'4` in $\mathbb{F}_5$ or `'4` in $\mathbb{Z}$) and simplifies the row requirement to:
   $$B_\omega(Z) \equiv (\texttt{= (+ '4 } Z\texttt{) '1})$$
   so child search never re-evaluates `(+ '2 '2)` on any candidate for $Z$. Conversely, if a pulled-back predicate contains a partial subexpression such as `(= (at '(5 6) Z) '5)` inside `(not ...)` or `(if ... 'nil 'nil)`, `provably-total-p` is `nil` so `fold-constants` leaves the partial branch intact, preserving exact `:error` vs. `'nil` outcomes on out-of-bounds $Z$.
2. **Immediate Conflict Pruning (`frame-conflict-p`)**: If instead a candidate decoder were $d(Z) = \texttt{(tuple } Z\; Z\texttt{)}$ against parent requirement `(= Y '(2 3))`, `fold-constants` decomposes the tuple equality by injectivity into `(if (= Z '2) (= Z '3) 'nil)` and propagates `Z = '2` into `(= '2 '3) -> 'nil`. `frame-conflict-p` immediately flags `:unsatisfiable-row` in $O(|\Omega|)$ time before child enumeration starts.
3. **Downward Propagation (`propagate-arithmetic-child`)**:
   - In $\mathbb{F}_5$, the addition solver $q(K, C) = C - K$ has both witness ($\Leftarrow$) and uniqueness ($\implies$) laws `:validated` on $\mathbb{F}_5^2$. `propagate-arithmetic-child` evaluates $q(4, 1) \equiv 2 \pmod 5$, verifies $4 + 2 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, and replaces $B_\omega(Z)$ **in-place** with `(= Z '2)`.
   - In $\mathbb{Z}$, the solver $q(K, C) = C - K$ has status `:conjecture` (tested on $[-4, 4]^2$). `propagate-arithmetic-child` evaluates $q(4, 1) = -3$, forward-verifies $4 + (-3) = 1$, places `(= Z '-3)` in `propagation-frame` (`:primary`), and retains `(= (+ '4 Z) '1)` in `propagation-fallback` (`:fallback`) for 3:1 interleaved search in `propagation-search`.

### 3.3 Concrete Example 3: Unsupervised Concept Lifting via `infer-fibers`, `start-dependency-search`, and `condense-heuristic`

In Milestone 5 (`lifting-experiments.lisp`), observations of $y_{r,t} = a_{j(r)} + \iota(t)$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$ with unknown regime offsets $a_0 = 1, a_1 = 3$ and occurrence coordinate $r \in \{0, \dots, 2\cdot\text{reps}-1\}$ are pulled back through the decoder $d((r, t), Z) = \iota(t) + Z$:

1. **Bounded Fiber Partition Proposal (`infer-fibers`)**: On each row $\omega = (r, t)$, pulling back $A_\omega(Y) \equiv (\texttt{= } Y\;\texttt{'}y_{r,t})$ and applying M4 certified $\mathbb{F}_7$ addition propagation yields $B_\omega(Z) \equiv (\texttt{= } Z\;\texttt{'}a_{j(r)})$. `infer-fibers` tightens identical complete `row-known` tuples via `tighten-same-input-requirements` and runs bounded branch-and-bound lexicographic partition search to recover the $K = 2$ fiber classes $B_{c_0}(Z) \equiv (\texttt{= } Z\;\texttt{'1})$ and $B_{c_1}(Z) \equiv (\texttt{= } Z\;\texttt{'3})$, per-row labels $j_\omega \in \{0, 1\}$, computed intra-fiber `pairs` / cross-fiber `anchors`, and a status `message` (`:claim :partition-proposal`, `:feasibility :unresolved`).
2. **Explicit Dependency & Selector Synthesis (`start-dependency-search` and `condense-heuristic`)**: To determine which input coordinates or features predict the child concept or regime label $j_\omega$, `start-dependency-search` (`synthesize.lisp`) constructs competing child decompositions for each candidate dependency projection (such as $X_0 = r$ vs. $X_1 = t$ or synthesized features $e(r, t)$). The $t$-only projection immediately fails `frame-conflict-p` (`:incompatible-rows` since $t = 0$ requires both $1$ and $3$), whereas the $r$ projection is conflict-free and synthesizes either a paid `lookup-model` over $r$ (`context-label-frame`) or a compact intensional selector $\hat{\pi}(r, t)$ via `condense-heuristic` / `start-selector-search`.

### 3.4 Concrete Example 4: Debt-Draining VM Preemption and 3:1 Interleaved `propagation-search`

When a candidate expression performs an expensive operation—such as multiplying $2^{128} + i$ by itself on row $\omega$—`advance-evaluation` in `vm.lisp` computes the instruction tariff $2 + 129 \times 129 = 16{,}643$ work units and registers the excess over the remaining step budget in `evaluation-remaining-cost`. Subsequent calls with `quantum = 1` decrement `evaluation-remaining-cost` by 1 per quantum and charge `:proposal-execution` (or `:checker`) until the debt reaches 0, only then pushing the product onto `evaluation-stack`. Consequently, `propagation-search` can cleanly interleave 3 quanta on the narrowed `:primary` child job with 1 quantum on the un-narrowed `:fallback` job without ever losing sub-instruction work accounting or continuation parity.

### 3.5 Concrete Example 5: Resumable Recursive Synthesis & Parent-Level Pricing (`start-synthesis` in `synthesize.lisp`)

Suppose we want to synthesize a parent program on frame $\Omega_P$ where a direct root search (`start-search` on $\Omega_P$) competes against one or more candidate outer decompositions $Y = d_k(X, Z_k)$ (`make-decomposition` in `synthesize.lisp`):

1. **1-in-4 Root Reserve & Accounted Node Preparation**: `start-synthesis` / `resume-synthesis` maintains a root `search-job` alongside a queue of `synthesis-node`s. On every 4th quantum (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`), it advances the root `search-job` by 1 work unit; on the other 3 quanta, it advances the next active `synthesis-node`. When a node is in stage `:prepare`, `prepare-synthesis-node` runs `pullback-frame`, `frame-conflict-p`, and optional `propagate-arithmetic-child` inside an accounting wrapper (`with-work-limit`). If node preparation exceeds the current 1-unit slice, the excess work is stored in `synthesis-node-debt` (stage `:preparation`) and drained unit-by-unit before the child search job starts, guaranteeing exact `quantum = 1` vs. batch parity.
2. **Retaining All Child Candidates for Parent-Level Pricing**: A child expression $e_C$ that looks slightly more expensive locally inside $\Omega_C$ can be strictly cheaper after `compose-model` substitutes it into $d_k(X, Z_k)$ (or vice versa, when a child expression is duplicated multiple times inside $d_k(X, Z_k) = Z_k + (Z_k + Z_k)$). Therefore, `step-synthesis-node` checks **every** newly discovered child proposal in `search-job-proposals` (not merely the child's local best), reconstructs the full parent AST via `compose-model`, verifies it on $\Omega_P$, and updates `synthesis-job-best` using the true parent objective $S(M_{\mathrm{parent}}) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(M_{\mathrm{parent}})$.

### 4.1 Milestone 1: Core Typed Synthesis, Resumable Preemption, Reorientation, Pullback, Views & Aligned AU Controls {#sec-m1}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
Can a single size-bounded bottom-up enumerator (`search-job`) over a typed S-expression grammar $\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)$ synthesize compact programs $e$ satisfying $\forall \omega \in \Omega,\; A_\omega(M(e, \omega)) = \mathrm{t}$ (Operation 1.1) such that:

1. $J_{\mathrm{exact}}(e) = S(e) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(e)$ strictly beats both a packaged `lookup-model` table and a literal sequence packet on complete finite-field tables ($\mathbb{F}_5$) and generalizes to held-out $2^{256}$-scale inputs on unbounded integers ($\mathbb{Z}$);
2. Preemption and resumption at `quantum = 1` preserve exact continuation and work-ledger identity (`equalp`) with batch execution; and
3. Causal reorientation (`orient-observations`), contravariant pullback (`pullback-frame`), sequence factoring (`quotient-values`, `partition-rows`), and aligned anti-unification (`anti-unify`) operate on the exact same `frame` and AST representations without side-channel leakage?

#### Kernel Mechanics & Controls
- **Bottom-Up Size-Partitioned Enumeration (`enumerate.lisp`)**: `search-job` maintains a dynamic-programming `table` of synthesized expressions indexed by `(type . body-bits)`, a `cursor` tracking the current bit-size tier and operator production, and a resumable `active-eval` continuation (`candidate-eval`) for the expression currently being checked across `frame-rows`.
- **Positive & Negative Controls**:
  - *Cutoff vs. Exhaustion vs. Extension*: At bit bound $B = 50$ with small budget (`work-limit = 100`), search returns `:cutoff`; with sufficient budget at $B = 50$, search returns `:exhausted` with 0 solutions; extending the bound to $B = 61$ on the 25-row $\mathbb{F}_5$ table for $y = a + 2b$ (`gf5-fixture`) recovers `(+ X0 (+ X1 X1))` at $S = 59$ bits ($S_{\mathrm{header}} = 38$, $S_{\mathrm{body}} = 21$, $W_{\mathrm{run}} = 1{,}375$), beating the packaged `lookup-model` ($S = 315$ bits, $W_{\mathrm{run}} = 28{,}750$) and the raw literal sequence ($S = 103$ bits).
  - *Contradictory Same-Input Control*: A frame containing two rows with identical `row-known` `(1 1)` and conflicting requirements `(= Y '0)` and `(= Y '1)` must return `:exhausted` with 0 solutions and reject `lookup-model` construction.
  - *Supplied Pullback Control*: Pulling the 25-row $\mathbb{F}_5$ frame for $y = a + 2b$ back through the supplied outer decoder $d(X_0, Z) = X_0 + Z$ where the child schema exposes only $X_1$ (`b`) synthesizes the child program `(+ X1 X1)` and composes it into `(+ X0 (+ X1 X1))`.
  - *Integer Generalization Control*: On `integer-linear-fixture` ($y = a + 2b$ over $[-2, 2]^2 \subset \mathbb{Z}^2$, 25 rows) with unrestricted integer literals, search recovers `(+ X0 (+ X1 X1))` ($S = 44$ bits, $S_{\mathrm{header}} = 23$, $S_{\mathrm{body}} = 21$) and replays without error on 6 held-out signed starts around $\pm 2^{256}$ (`integer-held-out-starts`).

### 4.2 Milestone 2: Observational Equivalence, Action Invariants & Scoped Replay {#sec-m2}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
Can the system group enumerated expressions $e \in \mathcal{L}(\Gamma)$ by their empirical outcome vectors $M(e, \omega)_{\omega \in \Omega}$ into observational equivalence classes (`observation-bucket`s, Operation 1.2) so that:

1. Under a state transition action $T : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}$ observed through trajectory step pairs $(\omega, T\omega)$ and cross-orbit anchors, `observation-invariants` isolates informative conservation laws ($M(e, \omega) = M(e, T\omega)$ on `pairs` and non-constant on `anchors`) without a supervised target column;
2. Discovered invariants replay on fresh held-out transition pairs (including $2^{256}$ cancellation starts in $\mathbb{Z}$) and reject nearby non-invariant transitions; and
3. Accidental empirical agreements (such as $X_0 = X_0^2$ on $\{0, 1\}$) split cleanly when discriminating inputs are added, while preserving the original scope's snapshot unchanged?

#### Kernel Mechanics & Controls
- **Scoped Observational Bucketing (`observation-scope`, `observation-job` in `enumerate.lisp`)**: `start-observation-search` / `resume-observation-search` enumerates well-typed expressions up to `size-limit` and evaluates each expression across all rows of `observation-scope`. Only expressions that complete on every row without `evaluation-error` or cutoff are inserted into `observation-bucket`s keyed by `(type . values)`. Every bucket retains **all** member syntax trees (`observation-bucket-expressions`) ordered by description size, selecting the shortest expression as the canonical representative (`observation-bucket-representative`) without deleting alternatives.
- **Transition Reorientation Without Simulation (`transition-probes` in `experiments.lisp`)**: Converts recorded orbit trajectories into `pairs` and `anchors` frames by charging `:orientation` work and zero `:simulation` work.

### 4.3 Milestone 3: Guarded Laws (3A), AU Law-Corpus Compression (3B) & Checked Symbolic Rewriting (3C) {#sec-m3}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
1. **Milestone 3A (Guarded Law Discovery & Certification)**: Can cross-row equivalences $e_1 \sim_\Omega e_2$ and partial-domain equations be paired with synthesized zero-residual Boolean guards $G(X)$ (`propose-guarded-laws`, Operation 1.3–1.4) and exhaustively validated on $\Omega = D^\Gamma$ to issue `law-certificate`s, while grading sample-only survivals on $\mathbb{Z}$ or subsets of $\mathbb{F}_p$ as `:conjecture` and refuting invalid domain transfers (such as $X_0^5 = X_0$ on $\mathbb{F}_7$ or $\mathbb{Z}$, and $2 \cdot (X_0 / 2) = X_0$ on $\mathbb{Z}$)?
2. **Milestone 3B (AU Law-Corpus Compression)**: When discovered laws are represented as first-class quoted syntax values `(lit (:code inputs :bool) (if G (= lhs rhs) 't))`, can Operation 2 (`propose-law-compressions`) compress the law corpus via aligned anti-unification templates and lowered constructor lambdas (`compile-au-constructor`), achieving significant bit savings over literal concatenation and dictionary storage while reconstructing every law AST identically and re-validating every decoded law?
3. **Milestone 3C (Symbolic VM & Checked Rewriting Amortization)**: Can rewrite rules be executed as ordinary DSL programs over `(:code inputs sort)` values using `:code-*` VM primitives, learned via contravariant pullback (`pullback-frame`), and pre-validated once in `prepare-rewrite-library` so repeated search runs amortize validation cost while preserving total/error semantics (`provably-total-p`)?

#### Kernel Mechanics & Controls
- **Joint Equation & Guard Discovery (`laws.lisp`)**: Running `start-observation-search` with `:retain-outcomes t` records per-row `row-outcome` values and errors (`:error`). `propose-guard-problems` extracts partial equivalence pairs $(e_1, e_2)$ that agree on a non-empty proper support $\Omega_{\mathrm{ok}} \subsetneq \Omega$ and have at least one error or disagreement outside $\Omega_{\mathrm{ok}}$; `support-frame` builds the indicator target $\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{ok}}}(\omega)$, `start-guard-search` synthesizes $G(X)$ with $\varepsilon_\omega = 0$, and `propose-guarded-laws` validates $G(X) \implies e_1(X) = e_2(X)$.
- **Totality & Error Preservation in Guarded Rewriting (`apply-guarded-rewrite`, `provably-total-p`)**: A guarded law $G(\sigma) \implies \ell(\sigma) = r(\sigma)$ may rewrite a subterm $\ell(\sigma)$ to $r(\sigma)$ inside an enclosing `(if cond then else)` **only** when the enclosing branch condition syntactically matches $G(\sigma)$ and every subterm erased by replacing $\ell(\sigma)$ with $r(\sigma)$ satisfies `provably-total-p` (no unguarded `:/`, `:at`, `:lookup`, or `:gather`). Thus unsound rewrites such as `(* '0 (/ '1 '0)) -> '0` or `(- (/ '1 '0) (/ '1 '0)) -> '0` are rejected.
- **Amortized Rewrite Library (`prepare-rewrite-library` in `laws.lisp`)**: Validates each candidate rewrite rule exhaustively across $D^\Gamma$ (charging `:validation` work), checks `provably-total-p`, and caches the compiled `prepared-rewrite` in `*prepared-rewrite-cache*` only after the full validation budget is paid.

### 4.4 Milestone 4: Algebraic Relation Reorientation, Guarded Solver Discovery & Dual-Grade Propagation {#sec-m4}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
Can the system observe forward binary operations $f(a, b) = c$ for $+, -, \times, /$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$, $\mathbb{F}_7$, and $\mathbb{Z}$, reorient those forward records into left-operand ($f(Z, K) = C$) and right-operand ($f(K, Z) = C$) relational frames without enumerating inverse values in the host (`orient-arithmetic-requirements`), synthesize guarded witness solvers $q(K, C)$ and validity guards $G(K, C)$ (`discover-arithmetic-solver`), grade both witness ($\Leftarrow$) and uniqueness ($\implies$) implications (`validate-arithmetic-solver`), and deploy the resulting 8-rule `propagator-library` inside `propagate-arithmetic-child` and `propagation-search` (Operation 3.4)?

#### Kernel Mechanics & Controls
- **Forward Relation & Definedness Guard Synthesis (`observe-arithmetic-relation`)**: Executes $f(a, b)$ across the forward grid ($\mathbb{F}_p^2$ or $[-4, 4]^2 \subset \mathbb{Z}^2$), recording values and division-by-zero errors, and synthesizes the forward definedness guard (`b != 0` for `:/`, `'t` for `:+`, `:-`, `:*`).
- **Relational Reorientation Without Host Inverse Enumeration (`orient-arithmetic-requirements`)**: Reorients the forward records into target $Z$ given $(K, C)$ by retaining the forward relational requirement $R_f(K, Z, C)$ (conjoined across colliding $(K, C)$ rows via `conjoin-requirements`). Thus non-injective rows such as $0 \cdot Z = 0$ keep the full relational predicate `(= (* '0 Z) '0)` (satisfied by all $Z$), contradictory rows $0 \cdot Z = 1$ keep an unsatisfiable conjunction, and multi-valued integer division rows such as $Z / 2 = 1$ keep `(= (/ Z '2) '1)` (satisfied by $Z \in \{2, 3\}$).
- **Solver & Guard Synthesis (`discover-arithmetic-solver`)**: Searches for a witness expression $q(K, C)$ satisfying the reoriented rows on their solvable support, then synthesizes the exact solvability guard $G(K, C)$ via zero-residual guard search on the witness's support indicator—discovering `K != 0` for field multiplication/division and the self-composed divisibility guard `(if (= K '0) 'nil (= (* K (/ C K)) C))` for integer multiplication $K \cdot Z = C$.
- **Weak Addition Bootstrap (`:body-bound 24`)**: When solving $K + Z = C$ or $Z + K = C$ using a restricted grammar containing **only `:+` and `:*`** (no `:-` or `:/`), search discovers the additive inverse $C + (p - 1) \cdot K$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$ (`4 * K` in $\mathbb{F}_5$, `6 * K` in $\mathbb{F}_7$) and $C + (-1) \cdot K$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.

### 4.5 Milestone 5: Intermediate Concept Lifting via Supplied Contexts, Bounded Fiber Proposals & Dependency Selection {#sec-m5}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
When a family of observations $y_{r,t} = d\bigl((r, t),\, a_{j(r)}\bigr)$ depends on an unobserved intermediate concept $Z = a_{j(r)}$ indexed by a latent regime $j(r) \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ across occurrences $r$ and inner index $t$:

1. Can pulling the parent frame $\Omega_P$ back through $Y = d((r, t), Z)$ (`pullback-frame`) and projecting onto a supplied regime coordinate $j(r)$ lift the child synthesis problem to $Z = g(j)$ so that total description bits $S(\text{composed})$ beat `lookup-model` and raw literal sequences as repetitions grow?
2. When no regime labels $j(r)$ are supplied, can **bounded fiber partition proposals** (`infer-fibers`, Operation 2.3) intersect the pulled-back value fibers $\mathrm{Fiber}(\omega) = \{z \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_C} \mid B_\omega(z) = \mathrm{t}\}$ across rows, avoid greedy bridge merges via branch-and-bound lexicographic search, and compete alongside **explicit dependency/selector synthesis** (`start-dependency-search`, `start-selector-search`, `condense-heuristic`, Operation 4.1–4.2)?

- **Four-Part Experimental Architecture (`lifting-experiments.lisp`)**:
  1. *Supplied Context Lifting & Repetition Scaling (`run-m5-supplied-lift-and-repetitions`)*: On `gf7-lifting-fixture` ($y_{r,t} = a_{j(r)} + \iota(t) \pmod 7$ with $a_0 = 1, a_1 = 3$, $t \in \{0, \dots, 6\}$, metered conversion `gf7-iota-conversion`), pulling back through $d((r,t), Z) = \iota(t) + Z$ and projecting onto supplied $j \in \{0, 1\}$ synthesizes the child predictor $a_j = 1 + 2j$ (`(+ '1 (+ X2 X2))`) and composes it with the outer decoder. ### 4.6 Milestone 6: Typed Anti-Unification, Subexpression Correspondence Search & Validated-Law Alternate Syntax {#sec-m6}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
Can Operation 2 in the term algebra be extended from root-aligned trees (`anti-unify`) to:

1. **Unaligned Subgraph Correspondence Search (`discover-subexpression-abstractions`)**: Given a corpus of trees $\{T_i\}_{i=0}^{N-1}$ where a shared template $p$ is embedded at **unknown, varying subtree paths** $\pi_i$ inside distinct outer wrappers $T_i = W_i\bigl[\pi_i \gets \mathrm{Subst}(p, \theta_i)\bigr]$ (and some trees may be unrelated distractions with $\pi_i = \texttt{:none}$), can the system discover $(p, \{\pi_i\}, \{\theta_i\})$ and encode the corpus via a spliced pre-order packet (`corpus-abstraction-model`) that beats literal storage, whole-tree dictionary storage, and root-aligned AU?
2. **Law-Normalized Syntactic Abstraction**: When equivalent subtrees are obscured by algebraic identity wrappers (such as $e + 0$ or $e \cdot 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_5$), can normalizing the corpus through a `:validated` rewrite library (`prepare-rewrite-library`) expose a strictly tighter anti-unification template $p$ and smaller description length $S$ than raw syntactic AU, while prohibiting uncertified `:int` sample conjectures from altering syntax?

#### Kernel Mechanics & Controls
- **Five-Stage Progression (`abstraction-experiments.lisp`)**:
  - *Stages 1–3 (`run-m6-aligned-stages-and-controls`)*:
    - Stage 1 (Known Pattern): Reconstructs trees from a supplied template $p$ via `instantiate` and via a lowered `:code` constructor (`compile-au-constructor`) on `au-substitutions-frame`.
    - Stage 2 (First-Order Matching): Extracts per-tree bindings $\theta_i$ against a supplied pattern $p$ via `match-pattern` (enforcing repeated-hole equality and operator-hole signature compatibility), and shows that when the extracted bindings $\theta_i$ still share common structure, running `anti-unify` tightens the loose pattern to the exact LGG.
    - Stage 3 (Aligned Anti-Unification): Compares `anti-unify` against an independent recursive oracle `m6-reference-aligned-au` up to hole renaming (`patterns-isomorphic-p`), verifying operator holes, repeated value holes, and cross-type fallback.

### 4.7 Milestone 7: Autonomous View Discovery, Partition Reassembly & Locality Scheduling {#sec-m7}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
1. **Autonomous Boolean View Discovery (`synthesize-supplied-partition-view`, `discover-partition-views` in `synthesize.lisp`)**: Given a parent frame $\Omega$ whose target values follow a piecewise regime split across unknown input regions, can the system enumerate candidate Boolean guards $G(X)$ over a guard grammar under a single shared `with-work-limit` budget, deduplicate observationally equivalent and complement ($G$ vs. $\neg G$) splits via Operation 1 (`observation-bucket`s), prune conflicting branches in $O(|\Omega|)$ via Operation 3 (`frame-conflict-p`), synthesize per-branch child models (`:separate-parts` `(if G M_true M_false)` and `:shared-decoder` $d\bigl(X, \texttt{(if } G\; Z_{\mathrm{true}}\; Z_{\mathrm{false}}\texttt{)}\bigr)$ with optional M4 `solver` propagation), price **all** candidate child combinations at the parent level (`price-view-combinations`), and select the view that minimizes $J(M) = S(M) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(M)$?
2. **Physical Permutation Invariance & Ordered Reassembly (`shuffle-frame-rows`, `partition-rows`, `reassemble-partition`, `partition-model`)**: Does shuffling the physical storage order of rows in $\Omega$ while keeping their declared coordinates $\mathbf{x}_\omega$ and occurrence keys `row-key` leave synthesized models and reassembled sequences completely invariant, while rejecting target-value grouping when no runtime selector or paid lookup key is available?
3. **Locality-Prioritized Occurrence Scheduling (`compute-tree-locality-positions`, `schedule-occurrence-pairs` in `factor.lisp`)**: When searching for shared subexpression abstractions in large multi-branch trees (`locality-abstraction-fixture`), does prioritizing candidate occurrence pairs by structural tree distance (`:locality` schedule with a 1-in-4 canonical reserve `(= 3 (mod turn 4))` or an induced `:locality-key` expression) reduce discovery work compared to `:canonical` order when repeated subtrees are co-local (`:co-local`) or made co-local by an induced parity key (`:interleaved`), while the 1-in-4 canonical reserve prevents starvation when matching subtrees are placed at opposite ends (`:distant`)?

#### Kernel Mechanics & Controls
- **Guard Partition Enumeration, Computed Linkages, Independent Branch Modes & Parent-Level Combination Pricing (`evaluate-guard-partition` in `factor.lisp`, `synthesize-supplied-partition-view` & `discover-partition-views` in `synthesize.lisp`)**:
  - `evaluate-guard-partition` evaluates a Boolean guard $G(X)$ across all rows of $\Omega$ in the metered VM, splits $\Omega$ into projected `true-frame` and `false-frame`, records the occurrence-preserving `partition` via `partition-rows`, and computes a new `linked-frame` via `with-frame-linkage` / `partition-pairs-and-anchors` carrying intra-regime must-agree `frame-pairs` and cross-regime must-vary `frame-anchors`.
  - Both `synthesize-supplied-partition-view` and `discover-partition-views` wrap the entire guard enumeration, partition evaluation, conflict check, child synthesis, and parent reassembly inside a single `with-work-limit` (`:budget`) and return a scope-tagged status `message` as their final return value.
  - Filters out trivial splits (where `true-rows` or `false-rows` is empty) and canonicalizes complement symmetry `(G, ~G)` using canonical Boolean masks so each unordered 2-part split $\{\Omega_{\mathrm{true}}, \Omega_{\mathrm{false}}\}$ is evaluated at most once, choosing the shorter guard expression $S(G)$.
  - Evaluates `:separate-parts` and `:shared-decoder` **independently**: if `:separate-parts` has a cross-row conflict on the raw parent targets (for example, when $Y = X_1 + Z_r$ varies with $X_1$ while the child language only exposes $X_0$), `:shared-decoder` is still attempted after pulling back through $d(X, Z)$ and stripping $X_1$.
  - On tentative propagation branches, if the narrowed `propagation-frame` triggers `frame-conflict-p` while `propagation-fallback` is conflict-free, `propagation-branch-conflict-p` preserves the branch and searches `propagation-search`. Across non-propagated branches, `collect-branch-candidates` runs `start-synthesis` and passes all verified child candidates to `price-view-combinations`, which scores every `(true-child, false-child)` pair at the assembled parent level.
- **Locality Distance & 3:1 Interleaved Schedule (`compute-tree-locality-positions`, `schedule-occurrence-pairs`)**:
  - Assigns each tree a structural position (raw tree index $0 \dots N-1$, or generated coordinates / equivalence classes after evaluating a priced `:locality-key` expression in the metered VM, partitioning via `partition-rows` with `reassemble-partition` verification, and quotienting via `quotient-values` with `restore-quotient` verification).
  - Orders candidate cross-tree occurrence pairs by ascending generated-space distance ($\ell_1$ Manhattan distance $\sum_r |u_{a,r} - u_{b,r}|$ on tuple/vector coordinates, quotient equivalence class agreement on coarse keys, or $|p_a - p_b|$ on 1D positions), path-depth alignment $||\pi_a| - |\pi_b||$, and spine depth $|\pi_a| + |\pi_b|$ in the locality lane, interleaving 3 locality pairs with 1 canonical pair (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`).

### 4.8 Milestone 8: Recursive Synthesis Controller, Dependency Selection, Conflict Pruning & Heuristic Condensation {#sec-m8}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
Can the kernel compose Operations 2, 3, and 4 into a resumable recursive synthesis engine (`synthesize.lisp`) that:

1. **Milestone 8A (Recursive Multi-Step Reconstruction & Parent-Level Pricing)**: Chains `pullback-frame` + `fold-constants` + `propagate-arithmetic-child` across single- or multi-step decompositions $Y = d_1(X, Z_1),\; Z_1 = d_2(X, Z_2),\; \dots$ (`make-decomposition`, `start-synthesis`, `resume-synthesis`), interleaves root search (1 quantum) with decomposed child search (3 quanta), retains all child proposals, and prices every reconstructed candidate $d(X, e_C)$ at the root parent level?
2. **Milestone 8B (Resumable Preemption, Preparation Debt Draining & Explicit Dependency Search)**: Suspends and resumes parent/child search continuations (`synthesis-job`, `synthesis-node`, `heuristic-search`) at `quantum = 1` with exact `equalp` ledger and continuation parity (including debt draining across node preparation), and makes coordinate projections, empty-input constants, and synthesized feature expressions explicit competing hypotheses (`start-dependency-search`)?
3. **Milestone 8C (Checked Conflict Explanations & Pruning)**: Prunes dead decomposition branches before child enumeration whenever `fold-constants` and `frame-conflict-p` detect a row-level structural contradiction (`:unsatisfiable-row`) or a cross-row input collision (`:incompatible-rows`), while preserving tentative fallback lanes when only the narrowed primary frame conflicts?
4. **Milestone 8D (Learned Search Preferences via Residual-Priced Heuristic Condensation)**: Condenses empirical label tables or logged continuation returns via resumable `start-heuristic-search` / `condense-heuristic` (Operation 4) into compact symbolic predictors $\hat{\pi}$ that price residuals on the original frame requirements during training and drop the residual table at deployment?

### 4.9 Milestone 9: Self-Hosted Compositional Vocabulary & Library Retrieval {#sec-m9}

#### Research Question & Mathematical Formulation
Once the system has discovered and verified reusable functions $f_0, f_1, \dots, f_{L-1}$ across Milestones 1–8, can those functions be registered in the object language under **opaque symbol IDs** (`#:F0`, `#:F1`, ... carrying only their input-output type signature $(\tau_0, \dots, \tau_{m-1}) \to \tau$ and no human-chosen names) while the system synthesizes **compact symbolic descriptors and retrieval heuristics** $\hat{\pi}$ in its own DSL (`condense-heuristic`, Operation 4.4) to prioritize which library entries to test and compose on new partial I/O frames?

#### Kernel Mechanics, Controls & Concrete Verification
- **Opaque Library Registration (`ast.lisp`, `vm.lisp`, `enumerate.lisp`)**:
  - Learned functions (such as shifts $s_c(x) = x + c$, scalings $m_c(x) = c \cdot x$, squares $q(x) = x^2$, and linear combinations $f(a, b) = a + 2b$) are wrapped as `validated-program` entries (`prepare-program` in `vm.lisp`) and exposed in `language-operators` under opaque symbol tags `:f0, :f1, ...` whose encoding bit cost is determined solely by operator arity and table index.
- **Behavioral Probe Signature & Descriptor Condensation (`condense-heuristic`)**:
  - Each library entry $f_\ell$ is evaluated on a fixed canonical probe tuple $\mathbf{p} = (p_1, \dots, p_k)$ (e.g., $x \in \{0, 1, 2\}$), charging `:discovery` work to form a behavioral probe table mapping probe outputs $(f_\ell(p_1), \dots, f_\ell(p_k))$ and algebraic property bits (such as $f_\ell(0) = 0$, $f_\ell(1) - f_\ell(0) = f_\ell(2) - f_\ell(1)$) to library indices or structural parameter values $c$.
  - Calling `condense-heuristic` on the probe table synthesizes a compact symbolic descriptor $\hat{\pi}$ (for example, recovering the shift constant $c = f(0)$ for the family $s_c(x) = x + c$ or the slope $c = f(1) - f(0)$ for $m_c(x) = c \cdot x$) that predicts the relevant library primitive directly from a new task's probe observations.
- **3:1 Prioritized Retrieval with 1-in-4 Canonical Reserve**:
  - On a new synthesis frame $\Omega$ (including partial I/O frames where $f(0)$ is unobserved and must be inferred from differences or relational probes, and two-step compositions $f_{\ell_1}(f_{\ell_2}(x))$), the search driver schedules **3 quanta testing descriptor-ranked library candidates for every 1 quantum of canonical bottom-up enumeration** (`(= 3 (mod turn 4))`), charging all descriptor evaluation and candidate verification work to `W_search` and verifying every proposed composition on the full frame $\Omega$ plus held-out inputs.

## Appendix A: Architectural Evolution — From `plan4` and `plan5` through `plan6` to `plan7`

This appendix records the architectural evolution from `plan4` (the original 16-file specification) and `plan5` (its initial homoiconic refactor) through `plan6` to the post-audit 9-module architecture of `plan7`.

### A.1 What `plan5` Diagnosed Correctly in `plan4` (Retained in `plan6` and `plan7`)

`plan5` identified three major sources of architectural friction in `plan4`:

1. **Elimination of the Two-Level AST Schism**: In `plan4`, object-language programs were `defstruct` nodes while Milestone 3C quoted programs (`(:code ...)`) were nested data structures manipulated by custom `:code-*` instructions. `plan5` replaced both with **canonical homoiconic S-expressions** (`(+ X0 '1)`) backed by a pooled symbol table (`*typed-symbol-pool*`) and a weak `eq` metadata table (`*node-meta*`) caching type and prefix-free bit size in $O(1)$.
2. **Unification of Discovery Mechanisms**: `plan5` recognized that synthesis (M1), invariants (M2), laws and guards (M3A), AU compression (M3B/M6), algebraic inversion (M4), concept lifting (M5), and view discovery (M7) are not nine unrelated algorithms, but projections of a small set of operations over an observation matrix $M(e, \omega)$ and pulled-back requirement predicates $A_\omega(d(X, Z))$.
3. **Consolidation of the Codebase**: `plan5` collapsed `plan4`'s 16 fine-grained source files into a compact homoiconic kernel.

### A.2 Five Architectural Stress-Test Findings in `plan5` and Their Resolution in `plan6`

Stress-testing `plan5` against the authoritative kernel and test suite exposed five places where `plan5` over-simplified a mechanism in a way that would violate the cost model, the epistemic grading rules, or integer/multi-fiber soundness:

1. **Pullback Must Include Closed-Subtree Constant Folding (`fold-constants`), Not Pure Syntactic Substitution**:
   - *The flaw in `plan5`*: `plan5` defined pullback purely as syntactic substitution $B_\omega(Z) := A_\omega(d(\mathbf{x}_\omega, Z))$, deferring all simplification to M3/M4 rewrite rules.
   - *Why that failed*: First, in Milestone 4's expensive-context control $y = a^2 + g(b)$ with $a = 2^{128} + i$, pure syntactic substitution leaves `(* 'a 'a)` unsimplified inside $B_\omega(Z)$, forcing the checker to recompute $a^2$ on every candidate child program $g(b)$ instead of amortizing the known context once during pullback. Second, in Milestone 8C, pulling `(= Y '(2 3))` back through the diagonal decoder $d(Z) = \texttt{(tuple } Z\; Z\texttt{)}$ produces `(= (tuple Z Z) '(2 3))`; without constructor-injectivity folding (`(and (= Z '2) (= Z '3)) -> 'nil`), the structural conflict is invisible until child enumeration wastes budget.
   - *Resolution*: `pullback-frame` immediately runs `fold-constants` (evaluating closed $Z$-free subtrees once in the metered VM, decomposing tuple equalities by injectivity, and simplifying Boolean/membership identities) followed by `frame-conflict-p` (`:unsatisfiable-row` and `:incompatible-rows`).

2. **Certified In-Place Equivalence ($\iff$) vs. Tentative 3:1 Witness Propagation ($\Leftarrow$)**:
   - *The flaw in `plan5`*: `plan5` treated downward propagation uniformly as applying a verified M3 rewrite rule `(op K Z) = C -> Z = q(K, C)` to simplify $B_\omega(Z)$ in-place.
   - *Why that failed*: Over finite fields $\mathbb{F}_p$, an exhaustively `:validated` bijection (both witness $\Leftarrow$ and uniqueness $\implies$ certified on $D^\Gamma$) is a true logical equivalence ($\iff$) when its guard holds, so replacing $B_\omega(Z)$ **in-place** with `(= Z 'v_w)` is exact and loses zero solutions. Over $\mathbb{Z}$, however, (a) solvers validated on $[-4, 4]^2$ have status `:conjecture`, and (b) truncating integer division `Z / 2 = 1` has **multiple solutions** $Z \in \{2, 3\}$ (uniqueness is `:refuted`). Replacing `(= (/ Z '2) '1)` in-place with `(= Z '2)` destroys $Z = 3$ and causes coupled child problems (such as `Z / 2 = 1` and `Z / 3 = 1`) to fail (`tentative-failure-and-success-both-preserve-integer-alternatives` in `tests/propagation.lisp`).
   - *Resolution*: Operation 3.4 enforces **dual-grade propagation**: `:validated` ($\iff$) solvers narrow $B_\omega(Z)$ in-place, whereas `:conjecture` ($\Leftarrow$) solvers populate a narrowed `:primary` frame alongside an un-narrowed `:fallback` frame scheduled **3:1** in `propagation-search` with forward replay against `propagation-original`.

3. **Two-Stage Concept Lifting (`infer-fibers` + `condense-heuristic`) and Non-Greedy MDL Fiber Clustering**:
   - *The flaw in `plan5`*: `plan5` described M5 Hard-EM concept lifting as "just `quotient-values` applied to pulled-back requirement predicates," and suggested greedy pairwise intersection of compatible predicates.
   - *Why that failed*: Exact syntactic quotienting of $B_\omega(Z)$ only merges rows whose pulled-back predicates are already syntactically identical (failing whenever rows have overlapping set or relational requirements such as $\{1, 2\}$ and $\{2, 3\}$), while greedy pairwise intersection falls into the bridge-vs-leaf trap (`{1,2}, {2,3}, {1,4}, {3,5}` merging `{1,2}` and `{2,3}` to `{2}` and stranding `{1,4}` and `{3,5}` into $K = 3$ clusters instead of the optimal $K = 2$). Moreover, `quotient-values` produces an extensional index list $j_\omega$, whereas deploying a lifted concept on a multi-coordinate parent $(r, t)$ requires either a paid `lookup-model` over $r$ or an intensional selector expression $\hat{\pi}(r, t)$.
   - *Resolution*: `infer-fibers` (`factor.lisp`) performs same-input tightening, bounded branch-and-bound lexicographic MDL partition search over deduplicated requirement groups, and computed intra-fiber `pairs` and cross-fiber `anchors` (`partition-pairs-and-anchors`, `fiber-pairs-and-anchors`), followed by deployment via `context-label-frame` or selector synthesis (`condense-heuristic` / `start-selector-search`).

4. **The Single Resumable Debt-Draining Stepper (`evaluation`) in `vm.lisp`**:
   - *The flaw in `plan5`*: `plan5` suggested a ~100-line recursive evaluator charging `work` on each call.
   - *Why that failed*: A host-stack recursive `eval` cannot suspend mid-expression or mid-instruction when a single bignum multiplication $(2^{128})^2$ or prepaid `:gather` bounds check exceeds a small step quantum (`quantum = 1`), breaking the core preemption parity tests (`batched-search-preserves-continuations-and-costs`, `batched-checker-debt-and-errors`, `unpaid-instruction-error-after-batched-work`).
   - *Resolution*: `vm.lisp` retains the single explicit frame-stack state machine `evaluation` with `evaluation-remaining-cost` debt draining, used uniformly across all modules.

5. **Separating Exact Synthesis ($\varepsilon_\omega = 0$) from Residual-Priced Heuristic Condensation (Operation 4)**:
   - *The flaw in `plan5`*: `plan5` wrote a single global objective $J(M) = S(M) + S(\varepsilon(M, D)) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(M) + \lambda_{\mathrm{search}} W_{\mathrm{search}}(M)$ without distinguishing where non-zero residuals $\varepsilon$ are permitted or how $W_{\mathrm{search}}$ is attributed.
   - *Resolution*: Section 1.5 and Section 2.4 separate the **object-level model objective** $J(M)$ from the **meta-level procedural objective** $J_{\mathrm{procedure}}(P)$, require $\varepsilon_\omega \equiv 0$ for valid synthesis, laws, and guards (Operations 1–3), and use `condense-heuristic` (Operation 4) to price residuals during heuristic learning and drop $\varepsilon$ at deployment behind the 1-in-4 canonical reserve.

### A.3 Post-Audit Refactor from `plan6` to `plan7` (Tariff v9, `requirements.lisp`, and `synthesize.lisp`)

Following a comprehensive audit of the `plan6` codebase for scientific clarity, semantic soundness on partial expressions, and strict budget accounting, eight targeted refactors were executed and verified in `tests/refactor.lisp` (bumping `+tariff-version+` to `9`):

1. **9-Module Decomposition (`requirements.lisp` and `synthesize.lisp` Split)**:
   - Requirement constructors and conservative simplification (`fold-constants`, `conjoin-requirements`) were extracted from `frame.lisp` into `requirements.lisp` so `frame.lisp` handles only frames, rows, linkages, reorientation, pullback, composition, conflict detection, and model verification.
   - High-level search orchestration (`start-heuristic-search`, `condense-heuristic`, `dependency-proposals`, `start-selector-search`, `start-dependency-search`, `start-synthesis`, `synthesize-supplied-partition-view`, `discover-partition-views`) was extracted from `factor.lisp` into `synthesize.lisp`, leaving `factor.lisp` focused purely on structural and fiber factorization primitives.
2. **Conservative Partial-Safe `fold-constants` Without Built-In Table Inversion (`requirements.lisp`)**:
   - Previously, `fold-constants` could fold `(if c 'nil 'nil) -> 'nil` or simplify `:member` / conjunctions without checking `provably-total-p`, which could turn runtime `:error` into `'nil` or `'t` on partial subexpressions (`(/ '1 X0)` or `(at '(5 6) X0)`), and it included built-in host routines that inverted 1D and 2D literal `:at` tables into coordinate sets.
   - In `plan7`, `fold-constants` uses a single lexical `remaining` budget across walk steps and closed-subtree VM evaluations, leaves `:error`/`:cutoff` subtrees intact, gates all structural reductions on `provably-total-p`, filters `:member` conjuncts per mentioning sibling conjunct (preserving unrelated coordinate conjuncts and reassembling tuple equalities), and removes built-in `:at` table inversion so inverse lookup witnesses are synthesized honestly by search (`inverse-grid-witness-is-synthesized-and-serializable`).
3. **Making Dependency Selection an Explicit Synthesis Target (`factor.lisp`, `synthesize.lisp`)**:
   - Previously, `tighten-same-input-requirements` included a special fallback that scanned single-coordinate projections $X_r$ inside preprocessing whenever multi-input rows were unrepeated.
   - In `plan7`, `tighten-same-input-requirements` intersects **only** identical complete `row-known` vectors (preserving `'nil` on conflicts), while coordinate projections, empty-input constants, and synthesized feature expressions $e(X)$ (`:feature-language`) are explicit competing child decompositions in `dependency-proposals` and `start-dependency-search`.
4. **Honest Epistemic Grading & Bounds on `infer-fibers` (`factor.lisp`)**:
   - `infer-fibers` is now explicitly bounded by `with-work-limit` (`:budget`) and `:branch-limit`, seeds a valid singleton-group fallback before recursion (so `:branch-limit 0` still returns a valid partition), and returns a 5th `status` message (`:claim :partition-proposal` or `:cutoff`, `:feasibility :unresolved`) so bounded partition proposals are never conflated with globally certified proofs.
5. **Resumable, Original-Requirement `heuristic-search` (`synthesize.lisp`)**:
   - `condense-heuristic` now wraps `start-heuristic-search` / `resume-heuristic-search`, which steps an `observation-job`, checks candidates against the **original** frame requirements (rather than scoring against a single arbitrary witness when a row requirement is a multi-valued set or predicate), includes the residual-count header `(positive-bit-size (1+ (length residual)))`, and preserves `:cutoff` when interrupted.
6. **Parent-Level Candidate Pricing Across Decompositions and Views (`synthesize.lisp`)**:
   - Both `start-synthesis` and `discover-partition-views` / `synthesize-supplied-partition-view` now retain **all** verified child proposals from each child search and evaluate every reconstructed parent candidate under the parent objective $S(M_{\mathrm{parent}}) + \lambda_{\mathrm{run}} W_{\mathrm{run}}(M_{\mathrm{parent}})$, preventing locally small child expressions that bloat the parent AST from beating globally smaller parent reconstructions (`composition-prices-parent-instead-of-local-child`).
7. **Unified `with-work-limit` Budget Accounting & Independent View Modes (`synthesize.lisp`)**:
   - `synthesize-supplied-partition-view` and `discover-partition-views` execute under one shared `with-work-limit` covering guard search, partition evaluation, conflict checks, child synthesis, and parent verification, and try `:shared-decoder` even when `:separate-parts` has a raw-target conflict (`shared-decoder-survives-separate-branch-conflict`).
8. **Ordinary Expression Model Serialization (`codec.lisp`)**:
   - `lookup-model`, `quotient-model`, `partition-model`, and `corpus-abstraction-model` all populate a `:program` AST slot and serialize through `encode-model` / `decode-model` as ordinary typed object-language expressions without special model-packet header kinds.
